### 1. 萨里岛的花 / The flower of Saari

勒明凯宁又叫阿赫蒂、考科米耶利，是一个英俊的年轻人，脸颊红润，头发漂亮，可是性情放荡不羁，眼睛总是在少女们身上打转。他听说萨里岛上有一位美丽的少女，名叫基利基，被称为“萨里岛之花”，许多求婚者——太阳的儿子、月亮的儿子、星星的儿子，甚至遥远国度的王子——都被她拒绝了。勒明凯宁不顾母亲的劝阻，赶着雪橇来到萨里岛。岛上的少女们嘲笑他，说他是个穷小子，连马都喂不饱。勒明凯宁并不生气，他留在岛上，白天和少女们一起玩耍，晚上和她们一起跳舞，很快就和岛上所有的少女都成了朋友，只有基利基一个人对他不理不睬，从不正眼看他。可是勒明凯宁已经下定了决心：非娶到这朵萨里岛之花不可。他的另一个名字考科米耶利，意思是“心在远方的人”，正好说明了他的性情：他永远不肯安安稳稳地待在家里。母亲曾警告他，萨里岛的人会看不起一个外乡来的穷小子，可他偏偏要去试一试。

*Lemminkäinen, also called Ahti and Kaukomieli, was a handsome young man with ruddy cheeks and fine hair, but wild and reckless, his eyes always roving after the maidens. He heard that on the island of Saari lived a beautiful maiden named Kyllikki, called the flower of Saari, who had refused many suitors — the son of the sun, the son of the moon, the son of the stars, even princes from distant lands. Against his mother's warnings he drove his sledge to Saari. The island maidens mocked him as a poor fellow who could not even feed his horse. He did not take offence; he stayed on the island, played with the maidens by day and danced with them by night, and soon was friends with every maiden there — except Kyllikki, who would not even look at him. But Lemminkäinen had made up his mind: he would have the flower of Saari. His other name, Kaukomieli, means far-minded, and it fits him: he could never stay quietly at home. His mother had warned him that the people of Saari would look down on a poor stranger, but he had to try.*

### 2. 雪橇上的新娘 / The bride in the sledge

有一天傍晚，少女们在林间空地上跳舞，基利基跳在最前面。勒明凯宁赶着雪橇冲过来，一把抱起基利基，把她扔进雪橇，用鞭子一甩，飞驰而去。基利基又哭又喊，求他放她回家，说：放我回去吧，不然我就喊我的兄弟们来，他们会追上你，把你的头砍下来。勒明凯宁说：我的心是勇敢的，我的剑是锋利的，我不怕你的兄弟。基利基哭着说，他是一个只会打仗的穷汉。勒明凯宁就向她许诺：我发誓，从今以后再也不去打仗，哪怕金银堆成山也不去。你也要发誓，从今以后不再到村子里去跳舞，不管多么想去。基利基答应了。两人在乌戈面前立下誓约，回到勒明凯宁的家。母亲欢喜地迎接了这位美丽的儿媳。这两个誓言把两个性情相反的人拴在一起：一个是好斗的英雄，一个是爱热闹的少女。誓言立下了，危险却也一起埋下了。

*One evening the maidens were dancing in a forest glade with Kyllikki leading them. Lemminkäinen drove up in his sledge, seized Kyllikki, threw her into the sledge, cracked his whip and sped away. Kyllikki wept and cried out, begging to be let go home: 'Let me go, or I will call my brothers; they will overtake you and cut off your head.' He answered: 'My heart is brave and my sword is sharp; I do not fear your brothers.' She wept that he was a poor man who only knew how to fight. So he promised her: 'I swear never again to go to war, not even for mountains of gold and silver. And you must swear never again to go dancing in the village, however much you long to.' Kyllikki agreed. They swore their oaths before Ukko and went to Lemminkäinen's home, where his mother joyfully welcomed her beautiful daughter-in-law. The two vows bound together two people of opposite nature, a quarrelsome hero and a maiden who loved merrymaking; with the vows, the danger too was laid down.*

### 3. 破誓 / The broken vow

日子一天天过去，勒明凯宁常常出门去打鱼。有一天他出海很晚还没有回来，基利基一个人在家闷得慌，就违背誓言，偷偷跑到村子里去跳舞了。勒明凯宁的妹妹艾尼基看见了，等哥哥一回来，就把这件事告诉了他。勒明凯宁又气又恼，说：基利基破了她的誓言，那我也不必再守我的誓言了。我要去打仗，我要到北方的波赫约拉去，向那里的少女求婚。基利基哭着请求原谅，他却已经铁了心。他对母亲说：快把我的战衣拿来，把我的剑拿来，我要到波赫约拉去，那里有美丽的少女，还有许多巫师等着我去较量。一个誓言的破灭，就这样把这位英雄重新推上了危险的道路。艾尼基告诉哥哥的话很简单：基利基到别人的村子里去了，在那里和人一起跳舞。这一句话，就让勒明凯宁觉得自己也被誓言解脱了。母亲劝他说，誓言是两个人一起立的，破了一半也还剩一半，可他已经听不进去了。

*The days went by, and Lemminkäinen often went out fishing. One day he stayed out late on the water, and Kyllikki, weary of being alone at home, broke her vow and slipped off to the village to dance. Lemminkäinen's sister Ainikki saw her and told her brother as soon as he came home. Angry and hurt, he said: 'Kyllikki has broken her oath, so I need not keep mine. I will go to war; I will go north to Pohjola and woo a maiden there.' Kyllikki wept and begged forgiveness, but his mind was made up. He said to his mother: 'Bring my war-coat, bring my sword. I am going to Pohjola, where there are beautiful maidens and many sorcerers waiting for me to match my strength against them.' So the breaking of one vow set the hero back on the road of danger. Ainikki's words to her brother were simple: Kyllikki has gone to a strange village and danced there. That one sentence was enough for Lemminkäinen to think himself released from his own oath. His mother said that the vow had been made by two and half of it still stood, but he would not hear her.*

### 4. 流血的梳子 / The bleeding brush

母亲苦苦劝他：不要去波赫约拉，那里的拉普兰巫师会用歌声把你唱进火里、唱进冰里，你一个人去会丢掉性命的。勒明凯宁说：拉普兰的巫师们早就对我唱过歌了，我一点也不怕。母亲说：你有一个美丽的妻子在家，还要去别处找什么新娘？他不听，梳好了头，把梳子往墙上——一说是往炉边——一扔，说：当这把梳子流出血来的时候，就是我遭到不幸的时候。母亲和妻子哭着看他穿上铁甲，佩上宝剑，套好了马，赶着雪橇向北方出发了。他一路上念着咒语，祈求乌戈和森林的神灵保佑他，给他的剑和马增添力量。那把留在家中的梳子，从此成了母亲日夜守望的信号。在整部史诗里，勒明凯宁的母亲是最坚定、最勇敢的人物之一。儿子离开的那一刻，她就开始守望；后面的故事会证明，她的爱比亡者之河还要深。勒明凯宁却头也不回，他的心思已经全在北方。

*His mother begged him: 'Do not go to Pohjola. The Lapland sorcerers will sing you into fire and ice; alone you will lose your life.' He answered: 'The Lapland sorcerers have sung against me before; I do not fear them.' 'You have a beautiful wife at home,' she said. 'Why seek a bride elsewhere?' He would not listen. He combed his hair and flung the brush against the wall — or by the stove, in some tellings — and said: 'When this brush drips blood, misfortune has befallen me.' His mother and wife wept as he put on his iron mail, girded his sword, harnessed his horse and drove off north. All the way he chanted charms, praying Ukko and the spirits of the forest to guard him and give strength to his sword and horse. The brush left at home became the sign his mother watched day and night. In the whole epic Lemminkäinen's mother is one of the most steadfast and courageous figures. From the moment her son left she began to watch; the story will show that her love runs deeper than the river of the dead. Lemminkäinen never looked back; his mind was already in the north.*

### 5. 巫师与盲牧人 / The sorcerers and the blind herdsman

勒明凯宁来到波赫约拉，走进洛希的大厅，那里坐满了巫师和歌者，弹琴的弹琴，唱歌的唱歌。他们看见这个陌生人，都不理睬他。勒明凯宁就站在门口唱起歌来，歌声像火一样从他口中喷出，他把那些巫师一个个唱走了：有的唱进了荒野，有的唱进了湖里，有的唱进了激流，有的唱到了冰封的远方。最后，大厅里只剩下一个又老又瞎、戴着湿帽子的牧人，叫马尔卡哈图。勒明凯宁没有对他唱歌，反而嘲笑他说：你这个可怜的老头，我才不屑对你唱，你又瞎又丑，连自己的姐妹都敢欺负，连牛都看不好。老牧人受了这番羞辱，心里怀恨，悄悄走出大厅，来到图奥内拉的黑河边，等着勒明凯宁总有一天会从这里经过。马尔卡哈图这个名字，意思是“湿帽子”。勒明凯宁唱走了所有强大的对手，却偏偏轻视了这个最不起眼的老人，这一次轻视，后来要了他的命。

*Arriving in Pohjola, Lemminkäinen went into Louhi's hall, which was full of sorcerers and singers playing and singing; they ignored the stranger. He stood in the doorway and began to sing, and song poured from his mouth like fire. He sang the sorcerers away one by one: some into the wilderness, some into the lakes, some into the rapids, some to icebound distant places. At last only one was left in the hall, an old, blind herdsman in a wet hat, named Märkähattu. Lemminkäinen did not sing against him but mocked him: 'You wretched old man, I would not stoop to sing against you; you are blind and ugly, you have wronged your own kin and cannot even tend the cattle.' Shamed and full of hatred, the old herdsman slipped out of the hall, went to the black river of Tuonela, and waited there for the day Lemminkäinen would pass that way. The name Märkähattu means Wet-hat. Lemminkäinen sang away all his mighty rivals but slighted the humblest old man of all, and that slight would cost him his life.*

### 6. 希西的麋鹿 / The elk of Hiisi

勒明凯宁向洛希求娶她的女儿。洛希说：你已经有妻子了，可要是你能滑雪捉住希西的麋鹿，我就把女儿给你。勒明凯宁找到一个造雪橇板的巧匠，请他做了一副好滑雪板，一边做一边夸口：森林里没有一只野兽能逃过我的滑雪板。恶灵希西听见了，就用朽木、柳枝、草根和沼泽里的东西造出一头麋鹿，让它奔向拉普兰。勒明凯宁滑着雪追了上去，滑雪板在雪地上嘶嘶作响，像火一样飞快。那头麋鹿一路狂奔，冲进拉普兰人的家里，踢翻了锅子，弄得煮好的肉都掉进了灰里，拉普兰的妇女和孩子又哭又笑。勒明凯宁用力过猛，一脚踩下去，滑雪板啪的一声折断了，滑雪杖也断了，麋鹿逃得无影无踪。他站在雪地里，心里懊恼极了。为他做滑雪板的巧匠，原诗里叫考皮，又叫吕利基。希西是史诗里的恶灵，常常和另一个恶灵勒姆波一起，给英雄们制造麻烦。

*Lemminkäinen asked Louhi for her daughter. Louhi said: 'You have a wife already, but if you can catch the elk of Hiisi on skis, my daughter shall be yours.' He went to a skilled maker of skis and had a fine pair made, boasting as they were made that no beast in the forest could escape his skis. The evil spirit Hiisi heard, and made an elk out of rotten wood, willow twigs, roots and things of the swamp, and set it running towards Lapland. Lemminkäinen sped after it on his skis, which hissed over the snow as swift as fire. The elk ran wild, burst into the Lapps' huts and kicked over their kettles so that the meat fell into the ashes, and the Lapp women and children wept and laughed. Lemminkäinen thrust down so hard that his ski snapped, and his ski-pole too, and the elk vanished. He stood in the snow, bitterly vexed. The ski-maker is called in the poem Kauppi, also Lyylikki. Hiisi is an evil spirit of the epic, often paired with another, Lempo, in making trouble for the heroes.*

### 7. 森林之神 / The gods of the forest

勒明凯宁修好了滑雪板，这一次他不再夸口，而是向森林之神祈祷。他向森林之王塔皮奥和森林女主人米耶利基唱起祈祷的歌：森林的主人啊，美丽的森林之母啊，请打开你的金库，把那头麋鹿赶到我的路上来，我会用金子和银子报答你。他唱森林的美丽，唱森林之母穿着金色的衣裙，唱树木像盛装的人一样站立。他一连祈祷了三天，森林之神被他的歌声感动了，把那头麋鹿赶到了他面前。勒明凯宁终于捉住了希西的麋鹿，把它带回波赫约拉交给洛希，说：我已经做到了，把女儿给我吧。可是洛希又提出了第二个难题。在原诗里，这些祈祷占了很长的篇幅，保留了古代芬兰猎人向森林神灵祈求猎物的祷词；塔皮奥的国度叫塔皮奥拉，是森林中一个富足的王国。这一次，勒明凯宁靠的不是夸口，而是谦卑的祈求。

*Lemminkäinen mended his skis, and this time, instead of boasting, he prayed to the forest gods. He sang to Tapio, king of the forest, and Mielikki, its mistress: 'Master of the forest, fair mother of the woods, open your treasure-house and drive that elk into my path, and I will repay you with gold and silver.' He sang of the beauty of the forest, of its mother in golden garments, of the trees standing like people in festive dress. For three days he prayed, and the forest gods, moved by his song, drove the elk to him. At last he caught the elk of Hiisi, brought it to Pohjola and gave it to Louhi: 'I have done it; give me your daughter.' But Louhi set him a second task. In the poem these prayers run long, preserving the words with which old Finnish hunters asked the forest spirits for game; Tapio's realm, Tapiola, is a rich kingdom in the forest. This time Lemminkäinen succeeded not by boasting but by humble prayer.*

### 8. 火焰之马 / The fire-breathing horse

洛希说：你去把希西那匹棕色的骟马套上笼头，它在希西的原野上奔跑，鼻孔里喷着火，嘴里冒着烟。勒明凯宁带着金笼头和银马勒出发了。那匹马奔过来，鼻孔里喷出的火焰让人无法靠近。勒明凯宁向乌戈祈求：至高的乌戈啊，请降下冰雹和雪雨，落在这匹烈马的身上，浇灭它的火焰。乌戈降下了铁一样的冰雹，那匹马被冰雹浇灭了身上的火，勒明凯宁趁机走上前去，用温和的话哄它：好马啊，不要伤害我，低下你的头来，套上这金色的笼头吧，我不会用鞭子抽你。马低下了头，他给它套上了笼头，骑着它回到了波赫约拉。可洛希还是不肯把女儿给他，又说出了第三个难题，这一次的地方，比前两次都要可怕得多。这一次，他又一次学会了不靠蛮力，而是靠祈求和温和的话语。

*Louhi said: 'Go and bridle the brown gelding of Hiisi, which runs on Hiisi's meadows breathing fire from its nostrils and smoke from its mouth.' Lemminkäinen set out with a golden halter and a silver bridle. The horse came galloping, and the flames from its nostrils let no one near. Lemminkäinen prayed: 'Highest Ukko, send down hail and sleet on this wild horse and quench its fire.' Ukko sent down hail like iron, the fire on the horse was quenched, and Lemminkäinen stepped up and coaxed it gently: 'Good horse, do not harm me; bow your head into this golden halter. I will not strike you with the whip.' The horse bowed its head; he bridled it and rode back to Pohjola. Still Louhi would not give him her daughter, and named a third task, in a place far more terrible than the first two. Once again he succeeded not by force but by prayer and gentle words.*

### 9. 图奥内拉的天鹅 / The swan of Tuonela

洛希说：你去图奥内拉的黑河上，只射一箭，射死那里的天鹅，我就把女儿给你。图奥内拉是亡者之地，那条黑河就在亡者之王图奥尼的国度里。勒明凯宁背着弓箭，来到黑河边的瀑布旁，等着天鹅游过来。可是那个被他嘲笑过的盲眼牧人马尔卡哈图早就埋伏在河边了。他看见勒明凯宁走来，就从河水里抓起一条水蛇，像投矛一样投过去，水蛇穿过了勒明凯宁的左腋，刺穿了他的心脏和右肩。勒明凯宁倒在地上，痛苦地说：我真是疏忽啊，我没有问过母亲，没有学会对付水蛇的咒语。我知道三条咒语，却唯独不知道这一条。如果母亲在身边，她一定会告诉我该怎么办。这只在亡者之河上游动的天鹅，后来成了芬兰艺术中有名的形象，作曲家西贝柳斯的交响诗《图奥内拉的天鹅》就取材于此。

*Louhi said: 'Go to the black river of Tuonela, and with a single arrow shoot the swan there; then my daughter shall be yours.' Tuonela was the land of the dead, and the black river lay in the realm of Tuoni, lord of the dead. Lemminkäinen took his bow and arrows and went to the falls of the black river to wait for the swan. But the blind herdsman Märkähattu, whom he had mocked, lay in wait by the river. Seeing Lemminkäinen come, he drew a water-serpent from the river and hurled it like a spear; it passed under Lemminkäinen's left arm and through his heart and out at his right shoulder. Lemminkäinen fell and said in his agony: 'I was careless: I never asked my mother, never learned the charm against the water-serpent. I knew three charms but not this one. If my mother were here, she would tell me what to do.' The swan swimming on the river of the dead became a famous image in Finnish art; Sibelius's tone poem The Swan of Tuonela takes its subject from here.*

### 10. 黑河里的碎片 / The pieces in the black river

盲眼牧人把奄奄一息的勒明凯宁扔进了图奥内拉的黑河里，河水把他卷向汹涌的瀑布。亡者之王图奥尼的儿子——一个满身血污的人——站在河里，拿着刀把勒明凯宁砍成了好几块，扔进了图奥内拉深深的河水里，说：你就在这里躺着吧，带着你的弓和箭，去射河上的天鹅吧。一位英雄就这样消失在亡者之河的深处，没有坟墓，也没有人知道他的下落。远方的家中，母亲和妻子还在等着他回来。这一章短促而阴暗，诗人只用几句话就交代了勒明凯宁的死，仿佛连诗歌本身也不愿在这黑河边多停留一刻。可是在《卡勒瓦拉》里，死亡并不总是故事的终点。原诗把图奥尼的儿子称为“血红的图奥尼之子”。那只天鹅仍在黑河上静静地游着，没有人射中它。

*The blind herdsman threw the dying Lemminkäinen into the black river of Tuonela, and the current swept him towards the roaring falls. The son of Tuoni, lord of the dead — a man stained with blood — stood in the river with a knife, hewed Lemminkäinen into pieces and threw them into the deep waters of Tuonela, saying: 'Lie there with your bow and arrows, and shoot the swan on the river.' So the hero vanished into the depths of the river of the dead, with no grave, and no one knowing where he lay. Far away at home his mother and wife still waited for him. The rune is brief and dark; the singer tells Lemminkäinen's death in a few lines, as if the song itself would not linger by that black river. But in the Kalevala death is not always the end of the story. The poem calls him the bloody son of Tuoni. The swan still swam quietly on the black river; no one had shot it.*

### 11. 母亲的寻找 / The mother's search

在勒明凯宁的家里，母亲每天都看着那把梳子。有一天，梳子上忽然渗出了血。母亲大哭起来，立刻提起裙子，一路奔向北方。她来到波赫约拉，逼问洛希：我的儿子在哪里？洛希先说给了他一匹马，又说让他去打猎了。母亲说：你在撒谎！快说实话，不然我就毁掉你的仓库，打碎你的三宝。洛希这才说出，她派勒明凯宁去射图奥内拉的天鹅。母亲到处寻找，她问树木，树木说：我们自己的苦已经够多了，没有看见你的儿子；她问道路，道路说：我被人踩、被车轧，没有看见；她问月亮，月亮也说不知道。最后她问太阳，太阳说：你的儿子在图奥内拉的黑河里，他被砍成了碎块，沉在河底。太阳高高在天上，什么都看得见，所以只有它知道真相。母亲听了，不再哭泣，而是转身去找能帮她把儿子捞上来的人。

*At home, Lemminkäinen's mother watched the brush every day. One day blood suddenly oozed from it. Weeping, she gathered up her skirts and ran all the way north. In Pohjola she demanded of Louhi: 'Where is my son?' Louhi said first that she had given him a horse, then that she had sent him hunting. 'You lie!' said the mother. 'Tell the truth, or I will destroy your storehouses and smash your Sampo.' Then Louhi admitted she had sent Lemminkäinen to shoot the swan of Tuonela. The mother searched everywhere. She asked the trees, and the trees said: 'We have troubles enough of our own; we have not seen your son.' She asked the road, and the road said: 'I am trodden and crushed by wheels; I have not seen him.' She asked the moon, and the moon did not know. At last she asked the sun, and the sun said: 'Your son is in the black river of Tuonela, hewn to pieces, lying at the bottom.' The sun, high in the sky, sees everything, and so it alone knew the truth. Hearing it, the mother stopped weeping and turned to find someone who could help her bring her son up from the river.*

### 12. 铜耙与蜜蜂 / The copper rake and the bee

母亲哭着去找铁匠伊尔马里宁，请他打一把耙子：铜的耙齿要一百寻长，耙柄要五百寻长。伊尔马里宁为她打好了铜耙。母亲来到图奥内拉的黑河边，请太阳让亡者之地的人们都睡去，然后用铜耙在河里一遍遍地耙，耙出了儿子的衬衫，耙出了他的靴子和帽子，最后在河底耙出了儿子的身体，可还少了一只手、半个头和许多块骨头。她又耙了一遍又一遍，终于把所有的碎块都找齐了。她把碎块拼在一起，请掌管血脉的女神苏奥内塔尔用纺锤把断开的血管一根根接起来，把骨头接上骨头，把肉接上肉。可儿子还是不能说话。母亲就派一只小蜜蜂去取蜜膏，蜜蜂先在草地上采来蜜，没有用；又飞过海去取，还是不够；最后飞过了九重天，从造物主的屋子里取来了神的蜜膏。母亲把蜜膏涂在儿子身上，勒明凯宁醒了过来，说：我睡得多么久啊。母亲问他为什么还想去波赫约拉，他说那个少女还在等他。母亲劝他放弃，带着他回到了家乡。

*Weeping, the mother went to the smith Ilmarinen and asked for a rake with copper teeth a hundred fathoms long and a handle five hundred fathoms long, and Ilmarinen forged it. At the black river of Tuonela she asked the sun to put the people of the land of the dead to sleep, then raked the river again and again. She raked up her son's shirt, his boots and his hat, and at last his body from the bottom — but a hand was missing, half the head and many bones. She raked again and again until she had found every piece. She laid the pieces together and called on Suonetar, goddess of the veins, to join the severed veins with her spindle, bone to bone and flesh to flesh. Still her son could not speak. So she sent a little bee for honey. The honey it gathered from the meadows did not help; what it brought from over the sea was not enough; at last it flew beyond the nine heavens and brought the Creator's own honey from his house. The mother anointed her son, and Lemminkäinen woke and said: 'How long I have slept!' When she asked why he still wanted to go to Pohjola, he said the maiden was waiting for him; she persuaded him to give it up and took him home.*

### 1. 大气之女与鸭蛋 / The daughter of the air and the duck's eggs

在最古老的时候，有一位少女，她是大气的女儿，名叫伊尔玛塔尔。她独自在空旷的天空中住了很久很久，渐渐觉得寂寞，就从天上降下来，落到了无边无际的大海上。狂风吹起了海浪，海浪使她怀了孕，她成了“水之母”，在海上漂流了七百年，却始终生不出孩子来。她向至高的天神乌戈哀求，请他来帮助她摆脱痛苦。不久，一只美丽的鸭子在海面上飞来飞去，想找一个地方筑巢，却到处找不到陆地。伊尔玛塔尔从水里抬起膝盖，鸭子以为那是一座小岛，就落在她的膝盖上筑了巢，下了六个金蛋，第七个是铁蛋。鸭子孵着蛋，伊尔玛塔尔觉得膝盖越来越烫，像要被火烧着一样，忍不住动了一下，蛋就滚进海里，摔碎了。可是蛋的碎片并没有消失：下半部的蛋壳变成了大地，上半部变成了高高的天穹，蛋黄变成了太阳，蛋白变成了月亮，蛋壳上的斑点变成了星星，黑色的碎片变成了天上的云。

*In the oldest times there was a maiden, daughter of the air, named Ilmatar. She lived alone for ages in the empty spaces of the sky until she grew weary of it, and came down to the boundless sea. The wind raised the waves, and the waves made her with child; she became the Water-mother and floated on the sea for seven hundred years without giving birth. She cried to Ukko, the highest god, to free her from her pain. Soon a beautiful duck came flying over the waters looking for a place to nest, but there was no land anywhere. Ilmatar raised her knee from the water; the duck took it for a little island, built her nest on it and laid six golden eggs and a seventh of iron. As the duck brooded, Ilmatar felt her knee grow hotter and hotter until it seemed to burn, and she moved it; the eggs rolled into the sea and broke. But the pieces did not perish: the lower halves of the shells became the earth, the upper halves the high vault of heaven, the yolk the sun, the white the moon, the specks on the shells the stars, and the dark fragments the clouds of the sky.*

### 2. 万奈莫宁的诞生 / The birth of Väinämöinen

世界有了形状，伊尔玛塔尔又在海上漂了许多年，开始塑造大地的样子：她把手一指，就出现了岬角；用脚一踩，就有了鱼儿栖息的深坑；侧身一躺，就形成了平滑的海岸；她脚踏过的地方成了海湾，头枕过的地方成了港口。可是她的儿子还没有出生。万奈莫宁在母亲的腹中待了三十个夏天，又待了同样多的冬天，在黑暗中渐渐长大。他向太阳、月亮和大熊星祈求：请给我打开一条道路，让我走出这狭小的地方，去看看天空的太阳和月亮。可是没有回应。于是他自己用无名指推开了城堡的门，用左脚的脚趾拨开了骨头的门闩，从母亲的身体里爬了出来，一头扎进了海里。他在海浪中漂了好几年，终于来到一片无名的海岬，爬上了岸。这位后来被称为“永恒的歌者”的万奈莫宁，一出生就已经是一个老人了。

*With the world given shape, Ilmatar floated on for many years and began to form the land: where she pointed her hand, headlands rose; where she pressed with her foot, deep pools for the fish were made; where she lay on her side, smooth shores appeared; where her feet touched, bays were formed, and where her head rested, harbours. But her son had not yet been born. Väinämöinen stayed in his mother's womb thirty summers and as many winters, growing in the dark. He prayed to the sun, the moon and the Great Bear: open a way for me out of this narrow place, that I may see the sun and moon in the sky. No answer came. So he pushed open the castle gate with his ring finger, slid back the bony bolt with the toes of his left foot, crawled out of his mother's body and plunged headlong into the sea. He drifted on the waves for years until he came to a nameless headland and climbed ashore. Väinämöinen, later called the eternal singer, was already an old man at his birth.*

### 3. 播种与大橡树 / The sowing and the great oak

万奈莫宁上了岸，看见大地光秃秃的，什么也不长。他请来播种者桑普萨·佩勒沃伊宁，让他在大地上撒下种子：山上种松树，山丘种冷杉，沼泽长桦树，低地长赤杨，湿地长柳树，河岸长稠李。树木都长起来了，只有橡树不肯发芽。后来一位从海里来的巨人帮忙，把一颗橡子种了下去，这棵橡树长得太大了，树冠遮住了天上的云，挡住了太阳和月亮，大地一片黑暗。万奈莫宁祈求母亲派人来砍倒它。海里走上来一个小小的男人，只有拇指那么高，穿着铜甲；可一转眼，他就长成了顶天立地的巨人，挥斧三下，把大橡树砍倒了，阳光重新照到大地上。接着，万奈莫宁开始种大麦。一只山雀告诉他：要先烧林开荒。他砍倒了整片森林，只留下一棵白桦，让鸟儿有地方栖息；一只鹰为此感谢他，替他打火烧林。他撒下麦种，向乌戈祈雨，大麦就长了出来。

*Coming ashore, Väinämöinen saw that the land was bare and nothing grew. He called the sower Sampsa Pellervoinen to scatter seed over the earth: pines on the hills, firs on the knolls, birches in the swamps, alders in the lowlands, willows in the wet places, bird-cherries by the rivers. Every tree grew except the oak. Then a giant from the sea planted an acorn, and the oak grew so great that its crown hid the clouds and blocked the sun and moon, and the earth was dark. Väinämöinen prayed to his mother to send someone to fell it. A little man rose from the sea, no taller than a thumb, armoured in copper; in a moment he grew into a giant reaching the sky, and with three strokes of his axe he felled the oak, and the sunlight returned. Then Väinämöinen began to sow barley. A titmouse told him to burn the forest first. He felled the whole forest but left one birch for the birds to rest in; an eagle, thankful, struck fire to burn the clearing for him. He sowed the grain, prayed to Ukko for rain, and the barley grew.*

<sub>来源 / Source：Kalevala: The Epic Poem of Finland, tr. John Martin Crawford (1888) · Runes I–X · 《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第二章。</sub>

### 4. 约卡海宁的挑战 / Joukahainen's challenge

万奈莫宁的歌声和智慧传遍了四方。北方有一个年轻的歌手，名叫约卡海宁，听说有人唱得比他好，心里很不服气，不顾父母的劝阻，赶着雪橇去找万奈莫宁比试。两架雪橇在路上迎面相撞，缰绳缠在一起。约卡海宁开始炫耀自己的学问，说起鱼儿在哪里产卵、山上长着什么树、天地是怎样造成的，甚至说世界是他造的。万奈莫宁说：这些都是孩子和女人的学问，你所说的，你都不在场。约卡海宁恼羞成怒，要和他拔剑决斗。万奈莫宁不屑与他相斗，就唱起歌来。随着他的歌声，约卡海宁的雪橇变成了湖边的灌木，马变成了河边的石头，弓变成了水上的彩虹，箭变成了天上的鹰，他自己则一点点沉进了沼泽，泥水没过了腰，没过了胸，一直没到了下巴。这是《卡勒瓦拉》里有名的“唱歌比赛”：两位歌手比的不是力气，而是谁知道万物的起源；而在这部史诗里，知道一样东西的来历，就意味着拥有支配它的力量。

*The fame of Väinämöinen's songs and wisdom spread far and wide. In the north lived a young singer named Joukahainen, who could not bear to hear that anyone sang better than he. Against his parents' warnings he drove off in his sledge to challenge Väinämöinen. Their sledges met head-on on the road and their reins tangled. Joukahainen began to boast of his learning — where the fish spawn, what trees grow on the hills, how heaven and earth were made — and even claimed that he had made the world. Väinämöinen answered that this was the lore of children and women, and that Joukahainen had been present at none of it. Stung, the young man challenged him to fight with swords. Scorning to fight, Väinämöinen began to sing. As he sang, Joukahainen's sledge became bushes by a lake, his horse a stone by the river, his bow a rainbow over the water, his arrows hawks in the sky, and he himself sank little by little into a swamp, the mire rising to his waist, to his breast, to his chin. This is the Kalevala's famous singing contest: the two singers compete not in strength but in knowing the origins of things, and in this epic to know where a thing came from is to have power over it.*

### 5. 用妹妹换回性命 / A sister for a life

约卡海宁陷在沼泽里，吓坏了，开始求饶。他说：放了我吧，我给你两张好弓；万奈莫宁不要，继续往下唱。他又说：我给你两条船；我给你两匹快马；我给你满盔的金子和满帽的银子；我给你所有的田地和庄稼。万奈莫宁都不要，泥水越升越高。最后，约卡海宁说：我把妹妹艾诺许给你，让她给你打扫屋子、烤面包、织布，做你的妻子。万奈莫宁听了，心里非常欢喜，就坐在一块喜悦的石头上，把刚才的歌倒过来唱了一遍，约卡海宁从沼泽里升了上来，马、雪橇和弓箭也都恢复了原样。约卡海宁垂头丧气地回到家，告诉母亲他把妹妹许给了老万奈莫宁。母亲却高兴地说：这正是我一直盼望的事，能有那位大智者做女婿。只有艾诺听了，坐在台阶上哭了起来。一个少女的婚事，就这样在两个男人的较量中被定了下来，而她本人一句话也没有被问过。

*Sunk in the swamp, Joukahainen was terrified and began to beg. 'Let me go, and I will give you two fine bows.' Väinämöinen would not take them and sang on. 'I will give you two boats... two swift horses... a helmet full of gold and a hat full of silver... all my fields and harvest.' Väinämöinen refused them all, and the mire rose higher. At last Joukahainen said: 'I will give you my sister Aino, to sweep your house, bake your bread and weave your cloth, and be your wife.' At this Väinämöinen rejoiced; he sat down on a stone of joy and sang his song backwards, and Joukahainen rose out of the swamp, and his horse, sledge and bow returned to their own shapes. Joukahainen went home crestfallen and told his mother he had promised his sister to old Väinämöinen. But his mother was glad: it was what she had always hoped for, to have the great sage as her son-in-law. Only Aino, hearing it, sat down on the steps and wept. So a maiden's marriage was settled in a contest between two men, and no one had asked her a single word.*

<sub>来源 / Source：Kalevala: The Epic Poem of Finland, tr. John Martin Crawford (1888) · Runes I–X · 《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三至四章。</sub>

### 6. 艾诺的命运 / The fate of Aino

艾诺到林中去折桦树枝做浴帚，在那里遇见了万奈莫宁。老人说：少女啊，不要为别人戴珠子和十字架，只为我戴吧。艾诺扯下胸前的十字架、手上的戒指、颈上的珠子和头上的发带，扔在地上，哭着跑回家去。母亲劝她不要哭，叫她打开阁楼上的箱子，穿上最好的衣服，戴上金银首饰——那都是母亲年轻时织的、留给她出嫁用的。艾诺却说：我宁愿嫁给大海里的鲑鱼，也不愿做老人的拐杖。她穿上最好的衣服，离开家，一连走了好几天，来到海边。她看见三个少女在海里洗澡，就脱下衣服，游到海中的一块彩色岩石上。岩石却沉进了海底，艾诺也跟着沉了下去，再也没有回来。一只野兔把消息带回了她的家。母亲哭了很久，她的眼泪流成了三条河，河边长出了三棵白桦，桦树上落下三只杜鹃，一只唱着“爱啊”，一只唱着“新郎啊”，一只唱着“欢乐啊”。诗中母亲后来悔恨地说：做母亲的人啊，千万不要逼女儿嫁给她不喜欢的人。

*Aino went into the woods to break birch twigs for a bath-whisk and met Väinämöinen there. 'Maiden,' said the old man, 'wear your beads and cross for no one but me.' Aino tore the cross from her breast, the rings from her fingers, the beads from her neck and the ribbons from her hair, threw them on the ground, and ran home weeping. Her mother told her not to weep, but to open the chest in the loft and put on her finest clothes and her gold and silver — things her mother had woven and kept for her wedding when she was young. Aino said she would rather be the bride of the salmon in the sea than the staff of an old man. She put on her finest clothes, left home, walked for days, and came to the sea. Seeing three maidens bathing, she took off her clothes and swam out to a bright-coloured rock. The rock sank to the bottom, and Aino sank with it and never came back. A hare carried the news home. Her mother wept so long that her tears became three rivers; three birches grew by them, and on the birches sat three cuckoos, one calling love, one calling bridegroom, one calling joy. The mother later says in her grief: mothers, never force a daughter to marry a man she does not want.*

### 7. 鱼与失去的新娘 / The fish and the lost bride

万奈莫宁听说艾诺死了，日夜悲伤。他到海边去钓鱼，钓了很久，钓上来一条奇怪的鱼，它不像鲑鱼，不像鳟鱼，也不像任何他见过的鱼。他拿出刀来，想把它剖开做早饭，那条鱼却从他手里一跃而起，跳回了海里，在离船不远的水面上抬起头来说：我不是给你做早饭的鲑鱼。我就是艾诺，约卡海宁的妹妹，你想娶的那个少女。我本想做你的妻子，为你铺床、做饭，可你没有认出我来。说完，它就沉入了深海。万奈莫宁拼命撒网、垂钓，再也找不到那条鱼了。他悲伤地回到家里，觉得自己老了，再也没有欢乐了。这时，他已故的母亲从海浪下的坟墓里醒来，对他说：不要为约卡海宁的妹妹悲伤，到北方的波赫约拉去吧，那里有更美丽、更聪明的少女。

*Hearing that Aino was dead, Väinämöinen grieved day and night. He went to the sea to fish and after long fishing caught a strange fish — not a salmon, not a trout, not like any fish he had seen. He took out his knife to cut it up for breakfast, but the fish leapt from his hand back into the sea, raised its head from the water near the boat, and said: 'I am no salmon for your breakfast. I am Aino, Joukahainen's sister, the maiden you sought. I would have been your wife, made your bed and cooked your food, but you did not know me.' Then it sank into the depths. Väinämöinen cast his nets and lines again and again but never found that fish. He went home in sorrow, feeling old and joyless. Then his dead mother woke in her grave beneath the waves and said: 'Do not grieve for Joukahainen's sister. Go north to Pohjola, where there are maidens fairer and wiser.'*

### 8. 约卡海宁的箭 / Joukahainen's arrows

万奈莫宁骑上一匹轻得像豆秆一样的马，踏着海浪向北方的波赫约拉出发。约卡海宁却一直记恨着沼泽里的耻辱。他做了一张钢弓，配上蛇筋一样的弓弦，削好了箭，蘸上了毒，埋伏在路边等着。母亲劝他不要射，说万奈莫宁是大地的歌者，他死了，世上的欢乐和歌声都会消失。约卡海宁不听。他射出第一支箭，飞得太高，射进了云里；第二支箭飞得太低，钻进了地下；第三支箭射中了万奈莫宁胯下的马。马倒下了，万奈莫宁跌进了大海，被风浪卷走了。约卡海宁回家得意地告诉母亲：我射死了老万奈莫宁。母亲说：你做了一件坏事，你射死了大地上最好的歌者。约卡海宁的母亲说得没错：在《卡勒瓦拉》里，歌声就是力量，也是世间欢乐的来源。好在这位“永恒的歌者”并没有死，大海托着他，像当年他出生时一样，把他带向远方。万奈莫宁在海浪中漂流，无依无靠，这时他已经离家很远，离北方的波赫约拉却越来越近了。

*Väinämöinen mounted a horse as light as a pea-stalk and set off over the waves for northern Pohjola. But Joukahainen had never forgotten his shame in the swamp. He made a bow of steel, strung it with a string like a serpent's sinew, shaped his arrows and dipped them in poison, and lay in wait by the way. His mother begged him not to shoot: Väinämöinen was the singer of the land, and if he died, joy and song would vanish from the world. He would not listen. His first arrow flew too high, into the clouds; his second too low, into the earth; his third struck the horse beneath Väinämöinen. The horse fell, Väinämöinen tumbled into the sea, and the wind and waves carried him away. Joukahainen went home and boasted to his mother that he had killed old Väinämöinen. 'You have done an evil thing,' she said. 'You have shot the best singer on earth.' Joukahainen's mother was right: in the Kalevala song is power and the source of the world's joy. But the eternal singer did not die; the sea bore him up, as it had at his birth, and carried him far away. Drifting helpless on the waves, he was far from home and drawing ever nearer to the north.*

### 9. 鹰与洛希 / The eagle and Louhi

万奈莫宁在海上漂了许多天，又冷又饿，看不到岸。这时，一只巨大的鹰从天边飞来，它记得当年万奈莫宁烧林时为鸟儿留下了一棵白桦，就对他说：你曾经为鸟儿留下了栖息的树，现在我来报答你。它让万奈莫宁骑在背上，一直把他驮到北方的波赫约拉海边，放下他就飞走了。万奈莫宁在陌生的海岸上哭泣，哭声传到了波赫约拉的女主人洛希那里。缺了牙的洛希坐船来找到他，把他带回家里，给他吃喝，让他恢复了力气。万奈莫宁说他想回家。洛希说：我可以送你回去，还把女儿嫁给你，只要你为我锻造一件三宝：用天鹅的羽毛尖、不生育的母牛的奶、一粒大麦和夏天母羊的一缕羊毛把它造出来。万奈莫宁说：我不会锻造三宝，可是我家乡有一位铁匠伊尔马里宁，他锻造过天空的穹顶，他一定能做到。洛希答应了，给了他一架雪橇，叮嘱他回家的路上千万不要抬头看天。北方的波赫约拉在史诗里是一片寒冷而强大的土地，它的女主人洛希会魔法、有心计，后来成了卡勒瓦拉英雄们最难对付的对手。

*Väinämöinen drifted on the sea for many days, cold and hungry, with no shore in sight. Then a great eagle came flying from the edge of the sky. Remembering that when Väinämöinen burned the forest he had left a birch for the birds, it said: 'You left the birds a tree to rest in; now I will repay you.' It took him on its back and carried him to the shore of northern Pohjola, set him down and flew away. Väinämöinen wept on the strange shore, and his weeping reached Louhi, the mistress of Pohjola. The gap-toothed Louhi came in her boat, found him, took him home, fed him and restored his strength. He said he longed to go home. Louhi said: 'I will send you home and give you my daughter too, if you will forge me a Sampo from the tip of a swan's feather, the milk of a barren cow, a grain of barley and the wool of a summer ewe.' Väinämöinen answered: 'I cannot forge the Sampo, but in my land there is a smith, Ilmarinen, who forged the vault of the sky; he can.' Louhi agreed, gave him a sledge, and warned him not to look up at the sky on his way home. Pohjola, the north, is in the epic a cold and mighty land, and its mistress Louhi, skilled in magic and cunning, becomes the hardest adversary of the heroes of Kalevala.*

### 10. 彩虹上的少女与斧伤 / The maiden on the rainbow and the axe

万奈莫宁赶着雪橇往南走，忽然听见头顶传来织布机的声音。他忘了洛希的叮嘱，抬头一看，波赫约拉的美丽少女正坐在彩虹上，用金梭银线织着布。万奈莫宁请她坐到自己的雪橇上来，跟他回家。少女说，要她跟他走，他得先做几件事：用一把没有刃的刀劈开一根马鬃，把一个鸡蛋打成看不出的结；剥下石头的皮，从冰上砍下木桩而不溅起一点碎屑。万奈莫宁一件件都做到了。少女又说：你还要用我纺锤的碎片造一艘船，不用手推、不用膝顶，把它推下水去。万奈莫宁在一块蓝色的石头上造船，可是恶灵希西和勒姆波暗中作怪，让他的斧头一滑，砍进了自己的膝盖。鲜血像河水一样涌出来，淹没了草地和山丘。万奈莫宁会许多咒语，却唯独想不起止住铁伤的那一首，因为他不知道铁的起源。

*Driving south in his sledge, Väinämöinen heard the sound of a loom above him. Forgetting Louhi's warning he looked up, and there sat the beautiful maiden of Pohjola on the rainbow, weaving with a golden shuttle and silver thread. He asked her to come down into his sledge and go home with him. She said he must first do certain things: split a horsehair with a knife that had no edge, and tie an egg into knots no one could see; peel the bark from a stone, and cut stakes from the ice without splintering it. He did them all. Then she said: 'Build a boat from the fragments of my spindle and launch it without pushing it with hand or knee.' Väinämöinen set to building on a blue stone, but the evil spirits Hiisi and Lempo made his axe slip, and it cut into his knee. Blood gushed out like a river, flooding the meadows and the hills. He knew many charms, but not the one to stop a wound of iron, for he did not know the origin of iron.*

### 11. 铁的起源 / The origin of iron

万奈莫宁流着血，赶着雪橇去找能治伤的人。他走过一户户人家，最后在一座小屋里找到了一位白胡子老人。老人说：比这更大的伤我也治过。可你得先告诉我铁是怎么来的。万奈莫宁就讲起了铁的起源：最早的时候，三位少女在天上把乳汁洒向大地，黑色的乳汁变成了软铁，白色的乳汁变成了钢，红色的乳汁变成了生铁。铁害怕火，躲进了沼泽和泉水里，藏在狼和熊的脚印下面。铁匠伊尔马里宁找到了它，把它放进熔炉里锻炼，铁哀求他不要伤害它，答应永远不伤害自己的亲人。可伊尔马里宁在给钢淬火时，希西的鸟——一只胡蜂——飞来，把毒蛇的毒液、蚂蚁的酸液和蛤蟆的毒汁偷偷放进了淬火的水里，钢从此变得凶猛，背叛了誓言，连自己的兄弟都要砍伤。老人听完，大声斥责铁的罪恶，念起了止血的咒语，又派儿子去熬制药膏，替万奈莫宁包扎了伤口。伤口愈合了，万奈莫宁感谢乌戈，赶着雪橇回到了家乡。

*Bleeding, Väinämöinen drove on in search of a healer. He passed house after house until, in a small hut, he found a white-bearded old man, who said: 'I have stopped greater floods of blood than this. But first tell me the origin of iron.' So Väinämöinen told it: in the beginning three maidens sprinkled their milk from the sky over the earth; the black milk became soft iron, the white became steel, the red became hard iron. Iron, afraid of fire, hid in the swamps and springs, under the footprints of the wolf and the bear. The smith Ilmarinen found it and put it into his furnace, and the iron begged him not to harm it, promising never to wound its kin. But as Ilmarinen tempered the steel, Hiisi's bird, a hornet, came and slipped the venom of serpents, the acid of ants and the poison of toads into the tempering water, so the steel became fierce, broke its oath and would cut even its own brothers. When the old man had heard it, he loudly rebuked iron for its wickedness, spoke the charms that stop blood, and sent his son to boil a salve, and bound the wound. It healed; Väinämöinen thanked Ukko and drove home.*

### 12. 锻造三宝 / The forging of the Sampo

万奈莫宁回到家，劝铁匠伊尔马里宁到北方去锻造三宝、娶波赫约拉的少女。伊尔马里宁说：我不去那个吃人的阴冷地方。万奈莫宁就唱出一棵开着花的冷杉，树梢上挂着月亮，枝间停着大熊星，骗伊尔马里宁爬上去摘月亮；等他爬到树顶，万奈莫宁唱起一阵狂风，把伊尔马里宁连人带树卷走，一直吹到了波赫约拉。洛希热情地款待他，问他能不能锻造三宝。伊尔马里宁找到一块石头砌起熔炉，叫奴隶们拉风箱，把天鹅的羽毛尖、母牛的奶、大麦和羊毛放进火里。第一天，炉中出来一张金弓，可它每天都要一颗人头，他把它折断了；第二天出来一艘红船，它专要去打仗；第三天出来一头金角母牛，脾气太坏；第四天出来一把金犁，专犁别人的田地，都被他扔回了火里。他唤来四方的风，风吹了三天三夜，三宝终于成形了，它有一个五彩的盖子，一边磨出吃的粮食，一边磨出卖的粮食，一边磨出存的粮食，每天各磨一大斗。洛希非常高兴，把三宝锁进波赫约拉的石山里，上了九道锁，三宝的根扎进地下九寻深。可是少女说她还不想离开母亲的家，伊尔马里宁只好垂头丧气地坐上洛希给的船，回到了家乡。

*Back home, Väinämöinen urged the smith Ilmarinen to go north, forge the Sampo and win the maiden of Pohjola. Ilmarinen refused to go to that cold land that eats men. So Väinämöinen sang up a flowering fir with the moon in its crown and the Great Bear in its branches, and coaxed Ilmarinen to climb up and fetch the moon; when he reached the top, Väinämöinen sang up a whirlwind that swept Ilmarinen away and carried him to Pohjola. Louhi welcomed him and asked whether he could forge the Sampo. Ilmarinen built a furnace on a rock, set thralls to work the bellows, and put the swan's feather tip, the milk, the barley and the wool into the fire. On the first day a golden bow came out, but it wanted a head every day, so he broke it; on the second a red boat that wanted only war; on the third a heifer with golden horns, too ill-tempered; on the fourth a golden plough that ploughed other men's fields — all thrown back into the fire. He called the four winds, which blew for three days and nights, and at last the Sampo took shape, with a many-coloured lid; on one side it ground grain for eating, on another for selling, on a third for storing, a great measure each day. Louhi rejoiced and locked the Sampo in the stone mountain of Pohjola behind nine locks, its roots sunk nine fathoms deep. But the maiden said she would not yet leave her mother's house, and Ilmarinen went home crestfallen in the boat Louhi gave him.*
