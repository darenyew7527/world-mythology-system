## 阅读边界

英俊而鲁莽的勒明凯宁从萨里岛抢走了少女基利基，两人立誓：他不再去打仗，她不再去村里跳舞。基利基破了誓，勒明凯宁就要到北方的波赫约拉去另求新娘。母亲苦苦相劝，他把梳子往墙上一掷，说：梳子流血的时候，就是我遭难的时候。他在波赫约拉用歌声赶走了所有的巫师，只放过了一个又老又瞎、被他嘲笑的牧人。洛希给他出了三道难题：滑雪追捕希西的麋鹿，给希西喷火的马套上笼头，射死图奥内拉黑河上的天鹅。前两件他都做到了，第三件时，那个盲眼牧人在河边用一条水蛇刺穿了他的心，把他抛进黑河，亡者之王的儿子把他砍成了碎块。家中的梳子流出了血。母亲赶到北方，从洛希和太阳那里问出真相，请铁匠打了一把长柄铜耙，从黑河里一块块捞出儿子的身体，缝合血脉，又派蜜蜂从九重天外取来神的蜜膏，终于让儿子活了过来。

The handsome, reckless Lemminkäinen carries off the maiden Kyllikki from the island of Saari, and they vow — he never to go to war, she never to go dancing in the village. When she breaks her vow, he sets out for northern Pohjola to woo another. His mother begs him to stay; he flings his brush at the wall: when it bleeds, misfortune has befallen me. In Pohjola he sings all the sorcerers away but spares an old blind herdsman, whom he mocks. Louhi sets him three tasks: to catch the elk of Hiisi on skis, to bridle Hiisi's fire-breathing horse, and to shoot the swan on the black river of Tuonela. He does the first two; at the third, the blind herdsman pierces his heart with a water-serpent and throws him into the river, where the son of the lord of the dead hews him to pieces. At home the brush drips blood. His mother hurries north, learns the truth from Louhi and the sun, has the smith forge a long-handled copper rake, gathers her son's body piece by piece from the river, joins his veins, and sends a bee beyond the nine heavens for the Creator's honey, until her son lives again.

### 01 · 萨里岛的花 / The flower of Saari

勒明凯宁又叫阿赫蒂、考科米耶利，是一个英俊的年轻人，脸颊红润，头发漂亮，可是性情放荡不羁，眼睛总是在少女们身上打转。他听说萨里岛上有一位美丽的少女，名叫基利基，被称为“萨里岛之花”，许多求婚者——太阳的儿子、月亮的儿子、星星的儿子，甚至遥远国度的王子——都被她拒绝了。勒明凯宁不顾母亲的劝阻，赶着雪橇来到萨里岛。岛上的少女们嘲笑他，说他是个穷小子，连马都喂不饱。勒明凯宁并不生气，他留在岛上，白天和少女们一起玩耍，晚上和她们一起跳舞，很快就和岛上所有的少女都成了朋友，只有基利基一个人对他不理不睬，从不正眼看他。可是勒明凯宁已经下定了决心：非娶到这朵萨里岛之花不可。他的另一个名字考科米耶利，意思是“心在远方的人”，正好说明了他的性情：他永远不肯安安稳稳地待在家里。母亲曾警告他，萨里岛的人会看不起一个外乡来的穷小子，可他偏偏要去试一试。

Lemminkäinen, also called Ahti and Kaukomieli, was a handsome young man with ruddy cheeks and fine hair, but wild and reckless, his eyes always roving after the maidens. He heard that on the island of Saari lived a beautiful maiden named Kyllikki, called the flower of Saari, who had refused many suitors — the son of the sun, the son of the moon, the son of the stars, even princes from distant lands. Against his mother's warnings he drove his sledge to Saari. The island maidens mocked him as a poor fellow who could not even feed his horse. He did not take offence; he stayed on the island, played with the maidens by day and danced with them by night, and soon was friends with every maiden there — except Kyllikki, who would not even look at him. But Lemminkäinen had made up his mind: he would have the flower of Saari. His other name, Kaukomieli, means far-minded, and it fits him: he could never stay quietly at home. His mother had warned him that the people of Saari would look down on a poor stranger, but he had to try.

### 02 · 雪橇上的新娘 / The bride in the sledge

有一天傍晚，少女们在林间空地上跳舞，基利基跳在最前面。勒明凯宁赶着雪橇冲过来，一把抱起基利基，把她扔进雪橇，用鞭子一甩，飞驰而去。基利基又哭又喊，求他放她回家，说：放我回去吧，不然我就喊我的兄弟们来，他们会追上你，把你的头砍下来。勒明凯宁说：我的心是勇敢的，我的剑是锋利的，我不怕你的兄弟。基利基哭着说，他是一个只会打仗的穷汉。勒明凯宁就向她许诺：我发誓，从今以后再也不去打仗，哪怕金银堆成山也不去。你也要发誓，从今以后不再到村子里去跳舞，不管多么想去。基利基答应了。两人在乌戈面前立下誓约，回到勒明凯宁的家。母亲欢喜地迎接了这位美丽的儿媳。这两个誓言把两个性情相反的人拴在一起：一个是好斗的英雄，一个是爱热闹的少女。誓言立下了，危险却也一起埋下了。

One evening the maidens were dancing in a forest glade with Kyllikki leading them. Lemminkäinen drove up in his sledge, seized Kyllikki, threw her into the sledge, cracked his whip and sped away. Kyllikki wept and cried out, begging to be let go home: 'Let me go, or I will call my brothers; they will overtake you and cut off your head.' He answered: 'My heart is brave and my sword is sharp; I do not fear your brothers.' She wept that he was a poor man who only knew how to fight. So he promised her: 'I swear never again to go to war, not even for mountains of gold and silver. And you must swear never again to go dancing in the village, however much you long to.' Kyllikki agreed. They swore their oaths before Ukko and went to Lemminkäinen's home, where his mother joyfully welcomed her beautiful daughter-in-law. The two vows bound together two people of opposite nature, a quarrelsome hero and a maiden who loved merrymaking; with the vows, the danger too was laid down.

### 03 · 破誓 / The broken vow

日子一天天过去，勒明凯宁常常出门去打鱼。有一天他出海很晚还没有回来，基利基一个人在家闷得慌，就违背誓言，偷偷跑到村子里去跳舞了。勒明凯宁的妹妹艾尼基看见了，等哥哥一回来，就把这件事告诉了他。勒明凯宁又气又恼，说：基利基破了她的誓言，那我也不必再守我的誓言了。我要去打仗，我要到北方的波赫约拉去，向那里的少女求婚。基利基哭着请求原谅，他却已经铁了心。他对母亲说：快把我的战衣拿来，把我的剑拿来，我要到波赫约拉去，那里有美丽的少女，还有许多巫师等着我去较量。一个誓言的破灭，就这样把这位英雄重新推上了危险的道路。艾尼基告诉哥哥的话很简单：基利基到别人的村子里去了，在那里和人一起跳舞。这一句话，就让勒明凯宁觉得自己也被誓言解脱了。母亲劝他说，誓言是两个人一起立的，破了一半也还剩一半，可他已经听不进去了。

The days went by, and Lemminkäinen often went out fishing. One day he stayed out late on the water, and Kyllikki, weary of being alone at home, broke her vow and slipped off to the village to dance. Lemminkäinen's sister Ainikki saw her and told her brother as soon as he came home. Angry and hurt, he said: 'Kyllikki has broken her oath, so I need not keep mine. I will go to war; I will go north to Pohjola and woo a maiden there.' Kyllikki wept and begged forgiveness, but his mind was made up. He said to his mother: 'Bring my war-coat, bring my sword. I am going to Pohjola, where there are beautiful maidens and many sorcerers waiting for me to match my strength against them.' So the breaking of one vow set the hero back on the road of danger. Ainikki's words to her brother were simple: Kyllikki has gone to a strange village and danced there. That one sentence was enough for Lemminkäinen to think himself released from his own oath. His mother said that the vow had been made by two and half of it still stood, but he would not hear her.

### 04 · 流血的梳子 / The bleeding brush

母亲苦苦劝他：不要去波赫约拉，那里的拉普兰巫师会用歌声把你唱进火里、唱进冰里，你一个人去会丢掉性命的。勒明凯宁说：拉普兰的巫师们早就对我唱过歌了，我一点也不怕。母亲说：你有一个美丽的妻子在家，还要去别处找什么新娘？他不听，梳好了头，把梳子往墙上——一说是往炉边——一扔，说：当这把梳子流出血来的时候，就是我遭到不幸的时候。母亲和妻子哭着看他穿上铁甲，佩上宝剑，套好了马，赶着雪橇向北方出发了。他一路上念着咒语，祈求乌戈和森林的神灵保佑他，给他的剑和马增添力量。那把留在家中的梳子，从此成了母亲日夜守望的信号。在整部史诗里，勒明凯宁的母亲是最坚定、最勇敢的人物之一。儿子离开的那一刻，她就开始守望；后面的故事会证明，她的爱比亡者之河还要深。勒明凯宁却头也不回，他的心思已经全在北方。

His mother begged him: 'Do not go to Pohjola. The Lapland sorcerers will sing you into fire and ice; alone you will lose your life.' He answered: 'The Lapland sorcerers have sung against me before; I do not fear them.' 'You have a beautiful wife at home,' she said. 'Why seek a bride elsewhere?' He would not listen. He combed his hair and flung the brush against the wall — or by the stove, in some tellings — and said: 'When this brush drips blood, misfortune has befallen me.' His mother and wife wept as he put on his iron mail, girded his sword, harnessed his horse and drove off north. All the way he chanted charms, praying Ukko and the spirits of the forest to guard him and give strength to his sword and horse. The brush left at home became the sign his mother watched day and night. In the whole epic Lemminkäinen's mother is one of the most steadfast and courageous figures. From the moment her son left she began to watch; the story will show that her love runs deeper than the river of the dead. Lemminkäinen never looked back; his mind was already in the north.

### 05 · 巫师与盲牧人 / The sorcerers and the blind herdsman

勒明凯宁来到波赫约拉，走进洛希的大厅，那里坐满了巫师和歌者，弹琴的弹琴，唱歌的唱歌。他们看见这个陌生人，都不理睬他。勒明凯宁就站在门口唱起歌来，歌声像火一样从他口中喷出，他把那些巫师一个个唱走了：有的唱进了荒野，有的唱进了湖里，有的唱进了激流，有的唱到了冰封的远方。最后，大厅里只剩下一个又老又瞎、戴着湿帽子的牧人，叫马尔卡哈图。勒明凯宁没有对他唱歌，反而嘲笑他说：你这个可怜的老头，我才不屑对你唱，你又瞎又丑，连自己的姐妹都敢欺负，连牛都看不好。老牧人受了这番羞辱，心里怀恨，悄悄走出大厅，来到图奥内拉的黑河边，等着勒明凯宁总有一天会从这里经过。马尔卡哈图这个名字，意思是“湿帽子”。勒明凯宁唱走了所有强大的对手，却偏偏轻视了这个最不起眼的老人，这一次轻视，后来要了他的命。

Arriving in Pohjola, Lemminkäinen went into Louhi's hall, which was full of sorcerers and singers playing and singing; they ignored the stranger. He stood in the doorway and began to sing, and song poured from his mouth like fire. He sang the sorcerers away one by one: some into the wilderness, some into the lakes, some into the rapids, some to icebound distant places. At last only one was left in the hall, an old, blind herdsman in a wet hat, named Märkähattu. Lemminkäinen did not sing against him but mocked him: 'You wretched old man, I would not stoop to sing against you; you are blind and ugly, you have wronged your own kin and cannot even tend the cattle.' Shamed and full of hatred, the old herdsman slipped out of the hall, went to the black river of Tuonela, and waited there for the day Lemminkäinen would pass that way. The name Märkähattu means Wet-hat. Lemminkäinen sang away all his mighty rivals but slighted the humblest old man of all, and that slight would cost him his life.

### 06 · 希西的麋鹿 / The elk of Hiisi

勒明凯宁向洛希求娶她的女儿。洛希说：你已经有妻子了，可要是你能滑雪捉住希西的麋鹿，我就把女儿给你。勒明凯宁找到一个造雪橇板的巧匠，请他做了一副好滑雪板，一边做一边夸口：森林里没有一只野兽能逃过我的滑雪板。恶灵希西听见了，就用朽木、柳枝、草根和沼泽里的东西造出一头麋鹿，让它奔向拉普兰。勒明凯宁滑着雪追了上去，滑雪板在雪地上嘶嘶作响，像火一样飞快。那头麋鹿一路狂奔，冲进拉普兰人的家里，踢翻了锅子，弄得煮好的肉都掉进了灰里，拉普兰的妇女和孩子又哭又笑。勒明凯宁用力过猛，一脚踩下去，滑雪板啪的一声折断了，滑雪杖也断了，麋鹿逃得无影无踪。他站在雪地里，心里懊恼极了。为他做滑雪板的巧匠，原诗里叫考皮，又叫吕利基。希西是史诗里的恶灵，常常和另一个恶灵勒姆波一起，给英雄们制造麻烦。

Lemminkäinen asked Louhi for her daughter. Louhi said: 'You have a wife already, but if you can catch the elk of Hiisi on skis, my daughter shall be yours.' He went to a skilled maker of skis and had a fine pair made, boasting as they were made that no beast in the forest could escape his skis. The evil spirit Hiisi heard, and made an elk out of rotten wood, willow twigs, roots and things of the swamp, and set it running towards Lapland. Lemminkäinen sped after it on his skis, which hissed over the snow as swift as fire. The elk ran wild, burst into the Lapps' huts and kicked over their kettles so that the meat fell into the ashes, and the Lapp women and children wept and laughed. Lemminkäinen thrust down so hard that his ski snapped, and his ski-pole too, and the elk vanished. He stood in the snow, bitterly vexed. The ski-maker is called in the poem Kauppi, also Lyylikki. Hiisi is an evil spirit of the epic, often paired with another, Lempo, in making trouble for the heroes.

### 07 · 森林之神 / The gods of the forest

勒明凯宁修好了滑雪板，这一次他不再夸口，而是向森林之神祈祷。他向森林之王塔皮奥和森林女主人米耶利基唱起祈祷的歌：森林的主人啊，美丽的森林之母啊，请打开你的金库，把那头麋鹿赶到我的路上来，我会用金子和银子报答你。他唱森林的美丽，唱森林之母穿着金色的衣裙，唱树木像盛装的人一样站立。他一连祈祷了三天，森林之神被他的歌声感动了，把那头麋鹿赶到了他面前。勒明凯宁终于捉住了希西的麋鹿，把它带回波赫约拉交给洛希，说：我已经做到了，把女儿给我吧。可是洛希又提出了第二个难题。在原诗里，这些祈祷占了很长的篇幅，保留了古代芬兰猎人向森林神灵祈求猎物的祷词；塔皮奥的国度叫塔皮奥拉，是森林中一个富足的王国。这一次，勒明凯宁靠的不是夸口，而是谦卑的祈求。

Lemminkäinen mended his skis, and this time, instead of boasting, he prayed to the forest gods. He sang to Tapio, king of the forest, and Mielikki, its mistress: 'Master of the forest, fair mother of the woods, open your treasure-house and drive that elk into my path, and I will repay you with gold and silver.' He sang of the beauty of the forest, of its mother in golden garments, of the trees standing like people in festive dress. For three days he prayed, and the forest gods, moved by his song, drove the elk to him. At last he caught the elk of Hiisi, brought it to Pohjola and gave it to Louhi: 'I have done it; give me your daughter.' But Louhi set him a second task. In the poem these prayers run long, preserving the words with which old Finnish hunters asked the forest spirits for game; Tapio's realm, Tapiola, is a rich kingdom in the forest. This time Lemminkäinen succeeded not by boasting but by humble prayer.

### 08 · 火焰之马 / The fire-breathing horse

洛希说：你去把希西那匹棕色的骟马套上笼头，它在希西的原野上奔跑，鼻孔里喷着火，嘴里冒着烟。勒明凯宁带着金笼头和银马勒出发了。那匹马奔过来，鼻孔里喷出的火焰让人无法靠近。勒明凯宁向乌戈祈求：至高的乌戈啊，请降下冰雹和雪雨，落在这匹烈马的身上，浇灭它的火焰。乌戈降下了铁一样的冰雹，那匹马被冰雹浇灭了身上的火，勒明凯宁趁机走上前去，用温和的话哄它：好马啊，不要伤害我，低下你的头来，套上这金色的笼头吧，我不会用鞭子抽你。马低下了头，他给它套上了笼头，骑着它回到了波赫约拉。可洛希还是不肯把女儿给他，又说出了第三个难题，这一次的地方，比前两次都要可怕得多。这一次，他又一次学会了不靠蛮力，而是靠祈求和温和的话语。

Louhi said: 'Go and bridle the brown gelding of Hiisi, which runs on Hiisi's meadows breathing fire from its nostrils and smoke from its mouth.' Lemminkäinen set out with a golden halter and a silver bridle. The horse came galloping, and the flames from its nostrils let no one near. Lemminkäinen prayed: 'Highest Ukko, send down hail and sleet on this wild horse and quench its fire.' Ukko sent down hail like iron, the fire on the horse was quenched, and Lemminkäinen stepped up and coaxed it gently: 'Good horse, do not harm me; bow your head into this golden halter. I will not strike you with the whip.' The horse bowed its head; he bridled it and rode back to Pohjola. Still Louhi would not give him her daughter, and named a third task, in a place far more terrible than the first two. Once again he succeeded not by force but by prayer and gentle words.

### 09 · 图奥内拉的天鹅 / The swan of Tuonela

洛希说：你去图奥内拉的黑河上，只射一箭，射死那里的天鹅，我就把女儿给你。图奥内拉是亡者之地，那条黑河就在亡者之王图奥尼的国度里。勒明凯宁背着弓箭，来到黑河边的瀑布旁，等着天鹅游过来。可是那个被他嘲笑过的盲眼牧人马尔卡哈图早就埋伏在河边了。他看见勒明凯宁走来，就从河水里抓起一条水蛇，像投矛一样投过去，水蛇穿过了勒明凯宁的左腋，刺穿了他的心脏和右肩。勒明凯宁倒在地上，痛苦地说：我真是疏忽啊，我没有问过母亲，没有学会对付水蛇的咒语。我知道三条咒语，却唯独不知道这一条。如果母亲在身边，她一定会告诉我该怎么办。这只在亡者之河上游动的天鹅，后来成了芬兰艺术中有名的形象，作曲家西贝柳斯的交响诗《图奥内拉的天鹅》就取材于此。

Louhi said: 'Go to the black river of Tuonela, and with a single arrow shoot the swan there; then my daughter shall be yours.' Tuonela was the land of the dead, and the black river lay in the realm of Tuoni, lord of the dead. Lemminkäinen took his bow and arrows and went to the falls of the black river to wait for the swan. But the blind herdsman Märkähattu, whom he had mocked, lay in wait by the river. Seeing Lemminkäinen come, he drew a water-serpent from the river and hurled it like a spear; it passed under Lemminkäinen's left arm and through his heart and out at his right shoulder. Lemminkäinen fell and said in his agony: 'I was careless: I never asked my mother, never learned the charm against the water-serpent. I knew three charms but not this one. If my mother were here, she would tell me what to do.' The swan swimming on the river of the dead became a famous image in Finnish art; Sibelius's tone poem The Swan of Tuonela takes its subject from here.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第十四章。
> Claim: `claim.v0350.kal_2.09`

### 10 · 黑河里的碎片 / The pieces in the black river

盲眼牧人把奄奄一息的勒明凯宁扔进了图奥内拉的黑河里，河水把他卷向汹涌的瀑布。亡者之王图奥尼的儿子——一个满身血污的人——站在河里，拿着刀把勒明凯宁砍成了好几块，扔进了图奥内拉深深的河水里，说：你就在这里躺着吧，带着你的弓和箭，去射河上的天鹅吧。一位英雄就这样消失在亡者之河的深处，没有坟墓，也没有人知道他的下落。远方的家中，母亲和妻子还在等着他回来。这一章短促而阴暗，诗人只用几句话就交代了勒明凯宁的死，仿佛连诗歌本身也不愿在这黑河边多停留一刻。可是在《卡勒瓦拉》里，死亡并不总是故事的终点。原诗把图奥尼的儿子称为“血红的图奥尼之子”。那只天鹅仍在黑河上静静地游着，没有人射中它。

The blind herdsman threw the dying Lemminkäinen into the black river of Tuonela, and the current swept him towards the roaring falls. The son of Tuoni, lord of the dead — a man stained with blood — stood in the river with a knife, hewed Lemminkäinen into pieces and threw them into the deep waters of Tuonela, saying: 'Lie there with your bow and arrows, and shoot the swan on the river.' So the hero vanished into the depths of the river of the dead, with no grave, and no one knowing where he lay. Far away at home his mother and wife still waited for him. The rune is brief and dark; the singer tells Lemminkäinen's death in a few lines, as if the song itself would not linger by that black river. But in the Kalevala death is not always the end of the story. The poem calls him the bloody son of Tuoni. The swan still swam quietly on the black river; no one had shot it.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第十四章。
> Claim: `claim.v0350.kal_2.10`

### 11 · 母亲的寻找 / The mother's search

在勒明凯宁的家里，母亲每天都看着那把梳子。有一天，梳子上忽然渗出了血。母亲大哭起来，立刻提起裙子，一路奔向北方。她来到波赫约拉，逼问洛希：我的儿子在哪里？洛希先说给了他一匹马，又说让他去打猎了。母亲说：你在撒谎！快说实话，不然我就毁掉你的仓库，打碎你的三宝。洛希这才说出，她派勒明凯宁去射图奥内拉的天鹅。母亲到处寻找，她问树木，树木说：我们自己的苦已经够多了，没有看见你的儿子；她问道路，道路说：我被人踩、被车轧，没有看见；她问月亮，月亮也说不知道。最后她问太阳，太阳说：你的儿子在图奥内拉的黑河里，他被砍成了碎块，沉在河底。太阳高高在天上，什么都看得见，所以只有它知道真相。母亲听了，不再哭泣，而是转身去找能帮她把儿子捞上来的人。

At home, Lemminkäinen's mother watched the brush every day. One day blood suddenly oozed from it. Weeping, she gathered up her skirts and ran all the way north. In Pohjola she demanded of Louhi: 'Where is my son?' Louhi said first that she had given him a horse, then that she had sent him hunting. 'You lie!' said the mother. 'Tell the truth, or I will destroy your storehouses and smash your Sampo.' Then Louhi admitted she had sent Lemminkäinen to shoot the swan of Tuonela. The mother searched everywhere. She asked the trees, and the trees said: 'We have troubles enough of our own; we have not seen your son.' She asked the road, and the road said: 'I am trodden and crushed by wheels; I have not seen him.' She asked the moon, and the moon did not know. At last she asked the sun, and the sun said: 'Your son is in the black river of Tuonela, hewn to pieces, lying at the bottom.' The sun, high in the sky, sees everything, and so it alone knew the truth. Hearing it, the mother stopped weeping and turned to find someone who could help her bring her son up from the river.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第十五章。
> Claim: `claim.v0350.kal_2.11`

### 12 · 铜耙与蜜蜂 / The copper rake and the bee

母亲哭着去找铁匠伊尔马里宁，请他打一把耙子：铜的耙齿要一百寻长，耙柄要五百寻长。伊尔马里宁为她打好了铜耙。母亲来到图奥内拉的黑河边，请太阳让亡者之地的人们都睡去，然后用铜耙在河里一遍遍地耙，耙出了儿子的衬衫，耙出了他的靴子和帽子，最后在河底耙出了儿子的身体，可还少了一只手、半个头和许多块骨头。她又耙了一遍又一遍，终于把所有的碎块都找齐了。她把碎块拼在一起，请掌管血脉的女神苏奥内塔尔用纺锤把断开的血管一根根接起来，把骨头接上骨头，把肉接上肉。可儿子还是不能说话。母亲就派一只小蜜蜂去取蜜膏，蜜蜂先在草地上采来蜜，没有用；又飞过海去取，还是不够；最后飞过了九重天，从造物主的屋子里取来了神的蜜膏。母亲把蜜膏涂在儿子身上，勒明凯宁醒了过来，说：我睡得多么久啊。母亲问他为什么还想去波赫约拉，他说那个少女还在等他。母亲劝他放弃，带着他回到了家乡。

Weeping, the mother went to the smith Ilmarinen and asked for a rake with copper teeth a hundred fathoms long and a handle five hundred fathoms long, and Ilmarinen forged it. At the black river of Tuonela she asked the sun to put the people of the land of the dead to sleep, then raked the river again and again. She raked up her son's shirt, his boots and his hat, and at last his body from the bottom — but a hand was missing, half the head and many bones. She raked again and again until she had found every piece. She laid the pieces together and called on Suonetar, goddess of the veins, to join the severed veins with her spindle, bone to bone and flesh to flesh. Still her son could not speak. So she sent a little bee for honey. The honey it gathered from the meadows did not help; what it brought from over the sea was not enough; at last it flew beyond the nine heavens and brought the Creator's own honey from his house. The mother anointed her son, and Lemminkäinen woke and said: 'How long I have slept!' When she asked why he still wanted to go to Pohjola, he said the maiden was waiting for him; she persuaded him to give it up and took him home.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第十五章。

依据克劳福德 1888 年英译《卡勒瓦拉》第十一至十五章，按诗章顺序讲述。隆洛特在编排勒明凯宁的故事时合并了民间不同英雄的唱本，本篇讲述的是隆洛特的编排；勒明凯宁被砍碎的情节照原诗交代结果，不渲染细节。
