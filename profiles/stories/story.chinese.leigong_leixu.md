## 阅读边界

东汉思想家王充在《论衡·雷虚》里，记下了他那个时代人们对雷的种种看法，然后一一加以反驳。当时的人们相信：雷电劈断大树、毁坏房屋，是上天来取龙；雷电打死人，是因为那人有不为人知的过错，比如给人吃了不干净的食物，上天发怒，就用雷把他打死，隆隆的雷声就是天发怒的声音。画工们画雷的样子，是一串连在一起的鼓，又画一个像力士一样的人，叫做雷公，左手拉着连鼓，右手举着槌子，像在敲打的样子。王充指出，雷公既然没有翅膀，就不可能在天上飞；他又举出五条证据，说明雷其实是火：被雷击死的人须发烧焦、皮肤灼伤，尸体上有火的气味；雷电能烧毁房屋草木。他认为，雷是盛夏时阳气与阴气相争而激发出来的气，就像把水泼进冶炼的炉火里会发出巨响一样。

In the 'Lei Xu' ('Thunder: the Falsehood') chapter of his Lunheng, the Eastern Han thinker Wang Chong records the beliefs of his day about thunder and refutes them one by one. People believed that when thunder split trees and wrecked houses, Heaven was taking a dragon; when it killed a man, it was punishing a hidden fault — feeding people unclean food, say — and Heaven in its anger struck him dead, the rumbling being the sound of Heaven's wrath. Painters drew thunder as a string of linked drums, and beside them a man like a strongman, called Leigong, the Thunder Lord, pulling the drums with his left hand and raising a mallet in his right as if to strike. Wang Chong points out that Leigong, having no wings, could not fly in the sky; and he gives five proofs that thunder is really fire: those killed by it have singed hair and burned skin and smell of fire, and lightning burns houses and plants. Thunder, he argues, is the energy thrown off when yang and yin contend in high summer, just as water poured on a smelting fire bursts with a roar.

### 01 · 盛夏的雷电 / Summer thunder

王充在《雷虚》的开头写道：盛夏的时候，雷电来得又快又猛，劈断树木，毁坏房屋，有时还会打死人。世俗的人认为，劈断树木、毁坏房屋，是上天来取龙；而打死人，是因为那人有暗中的过错，比如给别人吃了不干净的食物，上天发怒，就用雷把他打死。那隆隆的雷声，就是上天发怒的声音，就像人生气时大声呵斥一样。王充说，世上不论是愚笨的人还是聪明的人，没有一个人不这样认为。可是，如果按照人间的道理来推论，这些都是虚妄的说法。这一段话保存了汉代人对雷的普遍信仰：雷电不是自然现象，而是上天意志的表现；它既能取走天上的龙，也能惩罚人间的罪过。在这种信仰里，雷带有强烈的道德色彩，人们畏惧雷，也就是畏惧上天的审判。直到今天，汉语里还有“天打雷劈”这样的说法，用来诅咒做了坏事的人，可见这种观念流传之久。王充写这篇文章，正是要挑战这种人人深信不疑的看法。

Wang Chong opens 'Lei Xu' thus: in high summer thunder and lightning come swift and fierce, splitting trees, wrecking houses and sometimes killing people. Common people believe that when trees are split and houses wrecked, Heaven is taking a dragon; and when a man is killed, it is for a hidden fault — having fed others unclean food, for instance — so that Heaven in anger strikes him dead. The rumbling is the sound of Heaven's anger, like a man shouting in rage. Wang says that in all the world, foolish or wise, no one thinks otherwise; yet reasoned out by human sense, these are false words. The passage preserves a common Han belief: thunder and lightning are not natural events but signs of Heaven's will, able both to take dragons up to the sky and to punish wrongdoing on earth. In this belief thunder carries a strong moral colour; to fear thunder is to fear Heaven's judgement. Even today Chinese has the curse 'may Heaven strike you with thunder' for those who do evil, showing how long the idea has lasted. Wang wrote this chapter to challenge a view everyone held without doubt.

### 02 · 天怒之说 / Heaven's anger

王充首先反驳“雷是天怒”的说法。他说：雷发作的时候，只是一股气、一种声音。它劈断树木、毁坏房屋的时候，也会打死人；打死人的时候，也会劈断树木、毁坏房屋。可是世人却偏偏说，劈断树木、毁坏房屋是上天取龙，打死人是惩罚暗中的过错。取龙是吉事，罚过是凶事，两者吉凶不同，却在同一时间、发出同一种声音，这是讲不通的。王充又说：主张这种说法的人认为，隆隆的雷声是上天发怒时的呵斥声。这个说法用来解释惩罚罪过还勉强说得通，用来解释取龙就不合适了。惩罚罪过，上天发怒是可以理解的；可是取龙的时候，龙又有什么过错，让上天对它发怒呢？王充的论证方法很有特点：他并不直接否认上天的存在，而是从人们自己的说法里找出矛盾，用对方的逻辑来驳倒对方。这种方法贯穿了整部《论衡》，“论衡”的意思，就是用天平来衡量各种言论的轻重真假。

Wang first refutes the idea that thunder is Heaven's anger. When thunder breaks out, he says, it is one breath, one sound. When it splits trees and wrecks houses it may also kill people, and when it kills people it may also split trees and wreck houses. Yet people say that splitting trees and wrecking houses is Heaven taking a dragon, and killing people is punishing a hidden fault. Taking a dragon is auspicious, punishing a fault is ominous; that two things so different should happen at the same moment with the same sound makes no sense. He goes on: those who hold this view say the rumbling is Heaven's angry shouting. That might suit the punishment of faults, but not the taking of dragons. For punishing faults Heaven's anger is understandable; but in taking a dragon, what fault has the dragon committed that Heaven should be angry with it? Wang's method is distinctive: he does not deny Heaven outright but finds contradictions in people's own claims and refutes them by their own logic. The method runs through the whole Lunheng — whose title means 'weighing discourses' on a balance to test their weight and truth.

### 03 · 取龙与罚过 / Taking dragons and punishing faults

王充在《雷虚》中反复回到同一个问题：雷击中的人和物，究竟是被上天特意选中的，还是偶然碰上的？他在篇末的结论里说得很清楚：人在树下、在屋子里，偶然被雷打中，就死了。在他看来，这就像一个人走在路上，偶然被落下的石头砸中一样，并不是因为这个人有什么不为人知的罪过。至于“取龙”的说法，汉代人相信龙能升天，雷雨之时龙随着云气上升，所以把雷电劈开大树看作龙从树中飞出、被上天取走。王充在《论衡》的另一篇《龙虚》里专门讨论过这个问题，认为龙是一种生活在水中的动物，和鱼、蛇一类相近，它在雷雨中出现，是因为喜欢水和云气，并不是上天特意来取它。在《雷虚》里，他就这样把雷的“道德含义”一层层地剥去：雷打中的是偶然在场的人和物，它没有选择，也没有判断。这在当时是非常大胆的看法，因为汉代的许多学者都相信天能感应人事，用雷电、日食、地震等灾异来警告人间的君主和百姓。

In 'Lei Xu' Wang returns again and again to one question: are the people and things struck by thunder chosen by Heaven, or simply in the way? In the chapter's conclusion he says plainly: a man under a tree or in a house who happens to be struck by thunder dies. To him this is like a man on the road who happens to be hit by a falling stone; it is not because of some hidden fault. As for 'taking a dragon', Han people believed dragons could rise to heaven, ascending with the clouds in thunderstorms, so a tree split by lightning was seen as a dragon flying out of it and being taken up by Heaven. Wang discusses this in another chapter of the Lunheng, 'Long Xu' ('Dragons: the Falsehood'), holding that the dragon is a creature living in water, akin to fish and snakes, which appears in storms because it loves water and cloud, not because Heaven comes to take it. So in 'Lei Xu' he strips away thunder's moral meaning layer by layer: it strikes whatever people and things happen to be there, with no choice and no judgement. It was a bold view in its time, when many Han scholars believed Heaven responded to human affairs and warned rulers and people through portents such as thunder, eclipses and earthquakes.

### 04 · 画中的雷公 / Leigong in the paintings

王充接着描述了汉代画工所画的雷的样子：画工们画雷的形状，是一面接一面串在一起的鼓，像一串连鼓；又画一个人，有着力士一样的容貌，称他为雷公，让他左手拉着连鼓，右手举着槌子，像是正在敲打的样子。他们的意思是：雷声隆隆，就是连鼓互相撞击的声音；那突然像什么东西被撕裂一样的巨响，就是槌子敲打发出的声音；雷打死人，就是雷公拉着连鼓、举槌一齐敲打的结果。世人都相信这些画，没有人认为不对。王充说，如果追究其本原，这是虚妄的形象。这段文字非常珍贵，它是现存最早明确描写“雷公”形象的文献之一：一个力士般的人物，身边环绕着连鼓，手持槌子。这一形象在汉代画像石、画像砖上确实能够看到，后来又演变成民间和道教中长着鸟嘴、带着翅膀、手持锤钻的雷公。王充记录这一形象本是为了反驳它，却因此为后人保存下了汉代雷神信仰的面貌。

Wang then describes how Han painters drew thunder: painters picture thunder as drums strung together one after another, like a chain of linked drums; and they draw a man with the look of a strongman, whom they call Leigong, the Thunder Lord, making him pull the linked drums with his left hand and raise a mallet in his right, as if striking. Their meaning is that the rumbling of thunder is the sound of the linked drums knocking together; the sudden crack like something tearing is the sound of the mallet striking; and when thunder kills a man, Leigong is pulling the drums and striking with the mallet together. People believe these pictures, and none says otherwise. Traced back to its origin, Wang says, it is a false image. The passage is precious: it is one of the earliest surviving texts to describe the figure of Leigong explicitly — a strongman ringed by linked drums, mallet in hand. The figure can indeed be seen on Han carved stones and tiles, and later grew into the bird-beaked, winged Leigong with hammer and chisel of folk religion and Daoism. Wang recorded the image to refute it, and so preserved for us the face of Han thunder belief.

### 05 · 雷公怎样飞 / How could Leigong fly?

王充对画中的雷公提出了好几条质疑。他说：雷不是声音，就是气。声音和气，怎么能被拉住、推动，变成连鼓的样子呢？如果雷公真的存在，他敲鼓的时候，鼓要挂在什么地方才能响？他的脚又踩在什么地方？没有可以踩的地方，又怎么能打雷呢？王充又拿画中的仙人来比较：会飞的东西都有翅膀，没有翅膀却能飞的，人们称之为仙人，所以画工画仙人的时候，就给他画上翅膀，让他身上长毛，手臂变成翅膀。如果雷公和仙人一样能飞，就应该也给他画上翅膀；如果雷公不会飞，画工却说他在天上打雷，这就不对了；如果雷公真的会飞，却不给他画翅膀，这也不对。王充的这条论证很有意思：他用画工自己的绘画规则，指出了雷公形象的矛盾。有趣的是，后来的雷公画像果然长出了翅膀，在宋元以后的民间版画和寺观壁画中，雷公常被画成长着双翅的样子——也许正是为了弥补王充指出的这个“漏洞”。

Wang raises several objections to the painted Leigong. Thunder, he says, is either a sound or a breath (qi). How can a sound or a breath be pulled and pushed and made into linked drums? If Leigong really exists, where are the drums hung so that they sound when he strikes them? And where does he stand? With nothing to stand on, how could he make thunder? Wang compares the painted immortals: whatever flies has wings, and those who fly without wings people call immortals, so when painters draw immortals they give them wings — hair grows on their bodies and their arms become wings. If Leigong flies like the immortals, he too should be drawn with wings; if he does not fly, the painters are wrong to say he thunders in the sky; and if he truly flies but is drawn without wings, that is wrong too. The argument is clever: Wang uses the painters' own conventions to expose a contradiction in Leigong's image. Interestingly, later Leigong did grow wings: in folk prints and temple murals from the Song and Yuan onward he is often shown with two wings — perhaps to mend the very gap Wang pointed out.

### 06 · 雷是火 / Thunder is fire

王充提出了自己的看法：雷，是火。他举出五条证据来证明这一点。第一条：被雷打死的人，如果检查他的身体，打中头部的，胡须和头发都被烧焦；打中身体的，皮肤被灼伤；走近他的尸体，能闻到火的气味。这是第一条证据。第二条：懂得道术的人认为，雷把石头烧成红色，把烧红的石头投进井里，石头烫而井水冷，激发出巨大的响声，就像雷的声音一样。这是第二条证据。第三条：人受了寒气，寒气进入腹中，腹中原本温暖，温与寒相争，就激发出像雷一样的肠鸣。这是第三条证据。第四条：打雷的时候，闪电时时出现，大得像火光一样明亮。这是第四条证据。第五条：雷打下来的时候，有时会烧毁人的房屋和地上的草木。这是第五条证据。王充说，有这五条证据，就可以知道雷是火了。这五条证据，有的来自对雷击死者的观察，有的来自术士的实验，有的来自人体的感受，有的来自对闪电的观察，体现了一种难得的、重视经验与验证的思考方式。

Wang states his own view: thunder is fire. He gives five proofs. First: if you examine the body of a man killed by thunder, where the head was struck the beard and hair are singed, where the body was struck the skin is burned, and near the corpse you can smell fire. That is the first proof. Second: masters of the arts hold that thunder burns stones red; throw a red-hot stone into a well, and the hot stone and cold water clash with a great noise like thunder. That is the second proof. Third: when a man is chilled, the cold enters his belly; the belly being warm, warmth and cold contend and make a rumbling like thunder. That is the third proof. Fourth: when it thunders, lightning flashes again and again, bright as the glare of fire. That is the fourth proof. Fifth: when thunder strikes, it sometimes burns people's houses and the plants and trees on the ground. That is the fifth proof. With these five proofs, Wang says, we know that thunder is fire. Drawn from examining the struck dead, from the experiments of adepts, from bodily sensation and from watching lightning, the proofs show a rare way of thinking that values experience and verification.

### 07 · 炉中的水 / Water on the furnace

王充又用一个日常的经验来说明雷的威力：拿一斗水浇进冶炼铸造的炉火里，水气激荡，迸裂开来，发出的声音就像雷一样；如果有人靠得太近，一定会被灼伤身体。天地就是一个大火炉，阳气就是猛烈的炉火，云和雨就是大量的水；它们相争、激荡、迸射，怎么会不迅猛呢？打中了人的身体，人怎么会不死呢？这个比喻把雷电和汉代人熟悉的冶铁、铸铜工艺联系在了一起。汉代的冶铁业非常发达，许多人都见过炉火遇水时发出的爆裂声，王充用这个大家都能理解的现象来解释雷，让抽象的天象变得可以想象、可以检验。在他看来，雷之所以能杀人，不是因为雷公的槌子，也不是因为上天的愤怒，而是因为天地这个大炉子中，火与水相遇时那股巨大的力量。这种把宇宙看作一个巨大熔炉的想法，在中国古代思想中有着悠久的传统，贾谊的《鵩鸟赋》里就有“天地为炉兮，造化为工”的名句。

Wang also uses an everyday experience to show thunder's force: pour a peck of water onto the fire of a smelting furnace, and the steam bursts and cracks with a sound like thunder; anyone too close will surely be scorched. Heaven and earth are a great furnace, the yang energy is a fierce fire, the clouds and rain are a great deal of water; when they contend, clash and burst forth, how could it not be violent? And when it strikes a man's body, how could he not die? The comparison links thunder to the iron-smelting and bronze-casting the Han knew well. Han iron-working was highly developed, and many had heard the crack when furnace fire meets water; Wang uses a familiar sight to explain thunder, making an abstract heavenly sign something one can imagine and test. For him thunder kills not because of Leigong's mallet nor Heaven's anger but because of the immense force of fire meeting water in the great furnace of heaven and earth. The image of the cosmos as a vast furnace has a long tradition in Chinese thought; Jia Yi's 'Rhapsody on the Owl' has the famous line 'Heaven and earth are the furnace, creation the smith.'

### 08 · 太阳的激气 / The surging energy of great yang

在《雷虚》的结论部分，王充说：雷是太阳之气激发出来的。怎么证明呢？正月阳气开始萌动，所以正月开始打雷；五月阳气最盛，所以五月雷声最猛烈；秋冬阳气衰弱，所以秋冬雷就潜藏起来了。盛夏的时候，太阳之气当令，阴气却来侵犯它，阴阳相争，就互相摩擦撞击，摩擦撞击就会激射，激射就成为毒，打中人，人就死；打中树，树就折断；打中房屋，房屋就毁坏。人在树下或屋里，偶然被打中就死了。王充用阴阳二气的消长来解释雷的季节变化，这是汉代自然哲学的基本框架；可他与当时许多学者不同，他不认为天会有意地用雷来赏善罚恶。在这篇文章的最后，雷公这位汉代人画中的神，被王充一步步地还原成了一种自然的气。不过，雷公信仰并没有因此消失：后来的民间和道教把雷部众神发展成一个庞大的体系。王充的《雷虚》，既是对雷公信仰最早的详细记录，也是对它最早的系统批评，两者恰好保存在同一篇文字里。

In the conclusion of 'Lei Xu' Wang says: thunder is the surging energy of great yang. How can this be shown? In the first month yang begins to stir, so thunder begins in the first month; in the fifth month yang is at its height, so thunder is fiercest in the fifth month; in autumn and winter yang weakens, so thunder lies hidden. In high summer great yang holds sway, and yin encroaches on it; yin and yang contend and grind against each other; grinding, they burst forth; bursting, they become a poison — striking a man, it kills him; striking a tree, it breaks it; striking a house, it wrecks it. A man under a tree or in a house who happens to be struck dies. Wang explains the seasons of thunder by the waxing and waning of yin and yang, the basic frame of Han natural philosophy; but unlike many scholars of his day he denies that Heaven uses thunder on purpose to reward good and punish evil. By the chapter's end Leigong, the god of the Han paintings, has been step by step reduced to a natural energy. Yet belief in Leigong did not vanish: later folk religion and Daoism built a vast ministry of thunder gods. Wang Chong's 'Lei Xu' is both the earliest detailed record of the Leigong belief and its earliest systematic critique, preserved in one and the same text.

> 证据说明：王充《论衡·雷虚》雷者太阳之激气也。
