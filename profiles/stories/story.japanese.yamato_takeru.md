## 阅读边界

景行天皇的儿子小碓命性情凶猛，父亲害怕他，就派他去西方讨伐熊曾建。他借来姑母倭比卖的衣裳，扮成少女混进宴会，刺杀了熊曾兄弟，垂死的弟弟献给他“倭建”的名号。他在出云用木刀骗杀了出云建，回到京城，父亲又立刻派他去东方。他在伊势向姑母哭诉父亲想让他死，姑母给了他草薙剑和一个袋子。在相武的原野上，他用剑割草、用袋中的火石点起逆火，逃出火海；渡海时弟橘比卖投身海中平息风浪。他在足柄坂上叹息“吾妻哉”，在酒折宫与火守老人联句。回到尾张，他娶了美夜受比卖，却把草薙剑留在她身边，空手去讨伐伊吹山的神，被冰雹击伤。他拖着病体一路走一路唱，在能烦野唱着思念故乡大和的歌死去，化作一只白鸟飞向天空。

Keikō's son Wo-usu is so fierce that his father fears him and sends him west against the Kumaso bravoes. Disguised as a girl in his aunt Yamato-hime's robes, he kills the two brothers at their feast, and the dying younger brother gives him the name Yamato Takeru. He kills the Izumo bravo with a wooden-sword trick, returns, and is at once sent east. At Ise he weeps to his aunt that his father wishes him dead; she gives him the Kusanagi sword and a bag. On the moor of Sagamu he mows the grass and lights a counter-fire to escape the flames; at sea Oto-tachibana-hime gives herself to the waves to calm them. On the Ashigara pass he sighs 'Alas, my wife!', and at Sakaori he finishes a poem with an old fire-keeper. Back in Owari he marries Miyazu-hime but leaves the sword with her and goes bare-handed against the deity of Mount Ibuki, who strikes him with hail. Sick, he walks on singing, dies at Nobo singing of his longing for Yamato, and turns into a white bird that flies into the sky.

### 01 · 小碓命与兄长 / Wo-usu and his elder brother

景行天皇在缠向的日代宫统治天下，他有许多儿子，其中有一对兄弟：哥哥叫大碓命，弟弟叫小碓命，也叫倭男具那命。有一天，天皇对小碓命说：你哥哥为什么早晚都不来一起用膳？你去好好教导他。过了五天，大碓命还是没有来。天皇问小碓命：你哥哥怎么还不来？你是不是没有教导他？小碓命回答：我已经教导过了。天皇问：你是怎么教导的？小碓命说：清早他去厕所的时候，我等着把他抓住，折断了他的手脚，用草席包起来扔掉了。这段话在书中说得很直接，本篇只交代结果。天皇听了，对这个儿子凶猛暴烈的性情感到害怕。这位少年后来的名字叫倭建命，他的英雄故事，就从父亲的这份恐惧开始。书中在前面的部分还记了一件事：天皇曾派大碓命去美浓召两位少女进宫，大碓命却把她们据为己有，另找别的女子冒名献上；天皇心里明白，却一直闷闷不乐。

The Heavenly Sovereign Keikō ruled from the palace of Hishiro at Makimuku. Among his many sons were two brothers: the elder, Oho-usu, and the younger, Wo-usu, also called Yamato-woguna. One day the sovereign said to Wo-usu: 'Why does your elder brother not come to the morning and evening meals? Go and teach him.' Five days passed and still the elder brother did not come. The sovereign asked: 'Why does your brother still not come? Have you not taught him?' Wo-usu said he had. 'How did you teach him?' Wo-usu answered that he had waited for his brother at dawn when he went to the privy, seized him, broken his limbs, wrapped him in matting and thrown him away. The text tells it bluntly; this retelling gives only the outcome. The sovereign was afraid of his son's fierce and violent heart. The boy would later be called Yamato Takeru, and his hero's story begins with his father's fear. An earlier part tells that the sovereign had sent Oho-usu to summon two maidens of Mino to court, and that Oho-usu took them for himself and sent other women under their names; the sovereign knew and was troubled.

### 02 · 少女装扮 / Disguised as a girl

天皇说：西方有两个熊曾建，他们不肯归顺，没有礼数，你去把他们杀了。就派小碓命去了西方。那时小碓命还很年轻，头发在额前束成少年的发髻。他向姑母倭比卖命讨来她的上衣和裙子，又把一把短剑藏在怀里，出发了。到了熊曾建的家，只见房子四周围着三重军士，熊曾兄弟正在新建一座石室，准备举行落成的宴会，到处都是人声。小碓命在附近转了转，等着宴会的日子。到了那一天，他把束起的头发放下来梳成少女的样子，穿上姑母的衣裙，完全变成了一个少女，混在女人们中间，走进了石室。熊曾建兄弟一眼看中了这个少女，就让她坐在两人中间，一起饮酒作乐。倭比卖命是天皇的妹妹，在伊势侍奉天照大神；熊曾大致在今天九州的南部。一个束着少年发髻的皇子，就这样穿着姑母的衣裳，独自走进了敌人的宴席。

The sovereign said: 'In the west there are two Kumaso bravoes who will not submit and have no respect. Go and kill them,' and sent Wo-usu west. He was still young, his hair bound up at the forehead in a boy's knot. He begged from his aunt Yamato-hime her upper robe and skirt, hid a short sword in his bosom, and set out. At the Kumaso bravoes' house he found it surrounded by three rings of warriors; the brothers were building a new cave-dwelling for a housewarming feast, and the place was full of noise. Wo-usu walked about nearby, waiting for the day of the feast. When it came he let down his hair like a girl's, put on his aunt's robe and skirt, became in every way a young girl, mingled with the women and went into the dwelling. The two Kumaso bravoes were taken with the maiden at once, seated her between them, and feasted. Yamato-hime was the sovereign's sister, serving Amaterasu at Ise; Kumaso lay roughly in the south of present-day Kyushu. So a prince with a boy's hair-knot walked alone into his enemies' feast in his aunt's clothes.

### 03 · 倭建的名号 / The name Yamato Takeru

宴会正热闹的时候，小碓命从怀里拔出短剑，抓住哥哥熊曾建的衣领，一剑刺穿了他的胸膛。弟弟熊曾建吓得转身就逃，小碓命追到石室的台阶下，从背后抓住他，把剑刺了进去。弟弟熊曾建说：不要动这把剑，我有话要说。小碓命就按住他，听他说。熊曾建问：您是谁？小碓命说：我是统治大八岛国、住在缠向日代宫的大带日子淤斯吕和气天皇的儿子，名叫倭男具那王。天皇听说你们熊曾建两个不肯归顺、没有礼数，派我来杀你们。熊曾建说：确实是这样。在西方，没有比我们兄弟两个更勇猛强悍的人了；可是在大倭国，却有比我们更勇猛的男子。我要献给你一个名号：从今以后，你就叫倭建御子吧。说完，小碓命就把他杀了。从此，人们称他为倭建命。“建”在这里是勇猛的意思，“倭建”就是“大和的勇士”。他一生最有名的这个名字，竟是一位敌人临死前送给他的赞许。

When the feast was at its height, Wo-usu drew the sword from his bosom, seized the elder Kumaso bravo by the collar and thrust the sword through his chest. The younger bravo fled in terror; Wo-usu pursued him to the foot of the steps of the dwelling, caught him from behind and thrust the sword into him. The younger bravo said: 'Do not move the sword. I have something to say.' Holding him down, Wo-usu listened. 'Who is your augustness?' 'I am the son of the Heavenly Sovereign Ōtarashi-hiko-oshiro-wake, who dwells in the palace of Hishiro at Makimuku and rules the Land of the Eight Great Islands; my name is King Yamato-woguna. The sovereign heard that you two Kumaso bravoes would not submit and had no respect, and sent me to kill you.' The bravo said: 'It is true. In the west there are none so brave and strong as we two; but in great Yamato there is a man braver than we. I will offer you a name: henceforth be called the August Child Yamato Takeru.' When he had spoken, Wo-usu killed him. From then on he was called Yamato Takeru. Takeru here means brave: Yamato Takeru is the brave one of Yamato. The name he is best known by was a tribute from a dying enemy.

### 04 · 出云建与木刀 / The Izumo bravo and the wooden sword

倭建命回京的路上，把山神、河神和海峡之神一一平定，然后进入出云国，想要杀死出云建。他先和出云建结为朋友，又暗中用赤梼木做了一把假刀，佩在身上。有一天，两人一起在肥河里洗澡。倭建命先从河里出来，把出云建解下的刀佩在自己身上，说：我们来交换刀吧。出云建从河里出来，就佩上了倭建命的那把木刀。倭建命说：来，我们比比刀法吧。两人各自拔刀，出云建拔不出那把木刀，倭建命就拔出真刀，把出云建杀了。他唱了一首歌，大意是：出云建佩的那把刀，刀鞘上缠着一层又一层的藤蔓，看起来很好，里面却没有刀身，真是可怜啊。他平定了西方，回到京城，向天皇复命。这两件事，一靠扮成少女，一靠交换刀剑，都是用计取胜。同样的木刀计和同一首歌，在《日本书纪》里被放进另一个故事：出云的一对兄弟之间。两部书在这里各有各的讲法。

On his way back Yamato Takeru subdued the deities of the mountains, the rivers and the straits, and then went into Izumo intending to kill the Izumo bravo. First he made friends with him, and secretly made a false sword of ichii wood, which he wore. One day they bathed together in the river Hi. Yamato Takeru came out first, girded on the sword the Izumo bravo had taken off, and said: 'Let us exchange swords.' The Izumo bravo came out and girded on the wooden sword. 'Come,' said Yamato Takeru, 'let us cross swords.' Each drew; the Izumo bravo could not draw the wooden sword, and Yamato Takeru drew the real one and killed him. He sang a song whose sense is: the sword the Izumo bravo wore, its scabbard wound with creepers many times — fine to look at, but with no blade inside, alas. Having subdued the west, he returned to the capital and reported to the sovereign. Both deeds, the girl's disguise and the exchange of swords, were won by trickery. The same wooden-sword trick and the same song appear in the Nihon shoki in a different story, between two brothers of Izumo; the two books tell it each in their own way.

### 05 · 伊势的姑母 / The aunt at Ise

倭建命刚刚回来，天皇又命令他说：你去把东方十二道那些凶暴的神和不肯归顺的人平定了。天皇派吉备臣的祖先御锄友耳建日子跟他同行，又赐给他一支柊木做的八寻长矛。倭建命出发去东方，先到了伊势，参拜神宫，对在那里侍奉的姑母倭比卖命说：天皇是不是想让我死？他派我去打西方那些凶暴的人，我回来还没有多久，又不给我军队，就要派我去平定东方十二道的凶暴之人。这样想来，天皇一定是想让我死啊。他一边诉说，一边哭泣。临走时，倭比卖命把草薙剑交给他——这就是须佐之男从八岐大蛇尾中得到、天孙降临时带到人间的那把剑——又给了他一个袋子，说：如果遇到紧急的事情，就打开这个袋子。那支长矛，原文叫“比比罗木之八寻矛”，比比罗木就是柊树。至于袋子里装着什么，他当时并不知道。一个刚刚立下大功的皇子在姑母面前哭泣，这是《古事记》里少有的、写英雄软弱的一幕。

No sooner had Yamato Takeru returned than the sovereign commanded him again: 'Go and subdue the savage deities and unsubmissive people of the twelve roads of the East.' He sent with him Mi-suki-tomo-mimi-take-hiko, ancestor of the Kibi lords, and gave him a holly-wood spear eight fathoms long. On his way east Yamato Takeru went first to Ise, worshipped at the shrine, and said to his aunt Yamato-hime, who served there: 'Does the sovereign wish me dead? He sent me to strike the savage people of the west, and I have hardly come back before, without giving me any troops, he sends me to subdue the savage people of the twelve roads of the East. Thinking of this, I can only believe he wishes me to die.' He wept as he spoke. When he left, Yamato-hime gave him the Kusanagi sword — the sword Susanoo took from the serpent's tail and the August Grandchild brought down from heaven — and a bag, saying: 'If anything urgent happens, open the mouth of this bag.' The spear is called in the original an eight-fathom spear of hihiragi, the holly. What the bag held he did not yet know. A prince fresh from great deeds weeping before his aunt is one of the rare scenes in the Kojiki that show a hero's weakness.

### 06 · 相武的野火 / The fire on the moor of Sagamu

倭建命到了尾张国，进了尾张国造的祖先美夜受比卖的家，本想娶她为妻，转念一想，等回来时再娶吧，就和她立下婚约，继续往东去了。他把山河间凶暴的神和不肯归顺的人一一平定。到了相武国，那里的国造骗他说：这片原野里有一个大沼泽，住在沼泽里的神非常凶暴。倭建命就走进原野去看那位神。国造在原野上四面放火。倭建命知道上了当，打开姑母给的袋子，里面是一块打火石。他先用草薙剑割开身边的草，再用打火石点起一圈逆火，把烧来的大火挡了回去，平安地走了出来。他回过头来，把那些国造全部杀死，放火烧掉，所以那个地方至今叫作烧津。《日本书纪》说这把剑因为割草而得名“草薙”，而《古事记》在更早的大蛇故事里就已经这样称呼它了。书中把这件事记在相武国，而“烧津”这个地名今天在骏河，也就是静冈县，这一点历来被注释家讨论。

In Owari, Yamato Takeru entered the house of Miyazu-hime, ancestress of the rulers of Owari, and thought to marry her; then, deciding to marry her on his return, he pledged himself to her and went on east. He subdued the savage deities and unsubmissive people of the mountains and rivers one by one. In Sagamu the local ruler deceived him: 'In this moor is a great marsh, and the deity of the marsh is very violent.' Yamato Takeru went into the moor to see the deity, and the ruler set fire to the moor all round. Knowing he had been deceived, he opened his aunt's bag and found a fire-striker. First he mowed the grass around him with the Kusanagi sword, then lit a counter-fire with the striker and drove back the oncoming fire, and came out safe. He turned back, killed all those rulers and burned them, so that the place is still called Yaizu, Burning Ford. The Nihon shoki says the sword was named Kusanagi, Grass-Mower, from this deed; the Kojiki already calls it so in the earlier serpent story. The text places this in Sagamu, while the name Yaizu belongs today to Suruga, in Shizuoka — a discrepancy long discussed by commentators.

### 07 · 弟橘比卖投海 / Oto-tachibana-hime enters the sea

倭建命从那里出发，渡过走水海。海峡的神掀起大浪，船在原地打转，无法前进。这时，倭建命的妃子弟橘比卖命说：我代替皇子进入海中吧。皇子应当完成被派来做的事，回去向天皇复命。她要入海的时候，在浪上铺了八层菅草垫子、八层皮垫子和八层绸缎垫子，坐在上面，沉入了海中。汹涌的波浪自己平息下来，船又能前进了。弟橘比卖临走前唱了一首歌，大意是：在相武那群山耸立的小原野上，熊熊大火之中，你站在火里还在问我是否平安——啊，你啊。七天以后，她的梳子漂到了岸边。倭建命拾起那把梳子，为她修了陵墓，把梳子安放在里面。这首歌让人知道，在相武原野的大火里，她一直在丈夫的身边。走水海大致是今天东京湾口的浦贺水道一带，水流湍急，所以叫作“走水”。

From there Yamato Takeru crossed the sea of Hashirimizu. The deity of the strait raised great waves; the boat spun round and could not go forward. Then his consort Oto-tachibana-hime said: 'I will enter the sea in the prince's stead. The prince must accomplish the task he was sent on and report to the sovereign.' When she was about to enter the sea, she spread eight layers of sedge rugs, eight layers of skin rugs and eight layers of silk rugs on the waves, sat on them, and sank into the sea. The raging waves calmed of themselves and the boat could go on. Before she went she sang a song whose sense is: on the little moor of Sagamu among the standing peaks, in the midst of the burning fire, you stood and asked after me — ah, you. Seven days later her comb drifted ashore. Yamato Takeru took up the comb, made her a tomb and laid the comb in it. Her song shows that in the fire on the moor of Sagamu she had been at her husband's side. The Hashirimizu sea is roughly the Uraga channel at the mouth of Tokyo Bay, its current so swift that it was called Running Water.

### 08 · 足柄坂的白鹿 / The white deer on the Ashigara pass

倭建命继续深入东方，把凶暴的虾夷人都平定了，又平定了山河间凶暴的神，然后踏上了回程。来到足柄坂下，他正在吃干粮，那座山坡的神变成一只白鹿，走过来站在他面前。倭建命拿起吃剩的野蒜，等白鹿走近时扔过去，正好打中了它的眼睛，把它打死了。他登上足柄坂，回望来路，想起沉入海中的弟橘比卖，长叹了三声：吾妻哉！——“啊，我的妻子！”所以，书中说，从此人们就把那片土地叫作“阿豆麻”，也就是“东国”。这一声叹息，成了一个地方的名字。倭建命一路征战，从不诉苦，只在这里流露了一次深深的悲伤。虾夷指当时东方不服从朝廷的人们；足柄坂在今天神奈川与静冈交界的山上。白鹿被野蒜打中眼睛，这样细小的一笔，让这位山神的死显得格外突然。

Yamato Takeru went on deep into the East, subdued all the savage Emishi, pacified the savage deities of the mountains and rivers, and set out to return. At the foot of the Ashigara pass, as he was eating his dried rice, the deity of the pass changed into a white deer and came and stood before him. He took up the leftover end of the wild chive he had been eating, waited for the deer to come near, threw it, struck it in the eye and killed it. Then he climbed the pass, looked back the way he had come, remembered Oto-tachibana-hime who had sunk into the sea, and sighed three times: 'Azuma haya!' — 'Alas, my wife!' Therefore, says the text, the land has been called Azuma, the East. A sigh became the name of a land. Through all his fighting Yamato Takeru never complained; only here does he show his deep grief. The Emishi were the peoples of the East who did not submit to the court; the Ashigara pass lies in the mountains on the border of present-day Kanagawa and Shizuoka. The small detail of the wild chive striking the deer's eye makes the mountain deity's death all the more sudden.

> 证据说明：《古事记》张伯伦译本第八十五节。

### 09 · 酒折宫的火守老人 / The old fire-keeper at Sakaori

倭建命越过足柄坂，进入甲斐国，住在酒折宫里。有一天，他唱了一句歌：越过新治和筑波，已经睡了几夜？——意思是说，离开东方的新治与筑波以来，已经过了多少个夜晚了？身边的人没有一个能接下去。这时，一个在旁边看守火堆的老人接着唱道：数一数日子，夜是九夜，日是十日。倭建命十分赞赏这个老人，就封他为东国造。这一问一答的两句歌，后世被看作日本连歌的起源之一。在征战的长途中，这是少有的安静时刻：一位疲倦的皇子数着离家的日子，一位卑微的老人替他把日子数了出来。新治与筑波都在今天的茨城县，甲斐就是今天的山梨县，酒折宫的旧址在今天的甲府。身边的随从没有一个答得上来，答出这一句的，却是一个看火的老人。

Crossing from the pass into Kai, Yamato Takeru stayed at the palace of Sakaori. One day he sang a line: since passing Niibari and Tsukuba, how many nights have I slept? — that is, how many nights since leaving Niibari and Tsukuba in the East? None of his followers could answer it. Then an old man who was tending the fire took it up: counting the days, of nights there have been nine, of days ten. Yamato Takeru praised the old man and made him ruler of the Eastern Land. This exchange of two lines has been seen in later times as one of the beginnings of linked verse in Japan. On the long campaign it is a rare quiet moment: a weary prince counting the days away from home, and a humble old man counting them for him. Niibari and Tsukuba are in present-day Ibaraki, Kai is present-day Yamanashi, and the site of Sakaori is in today's Kofu. None of his attendants could answer; it was the old man watching the fire who did.

> 证据说明：《古事记》张伯伦译本第八十六节。

### 10 · 美夜受比卖与伊吹山 / Miyazu-hime and Mount Ibuki

倭建命从甲斐越过科野，平定了科野坂的神，回到尾张，进了美夜受比卖的家，履行当初的约定。两人以歌相答，结为夫妻。随后，他把草薙剑留在美夜受比卖的身边，空着手去讨伐伊服岐能山——伊吹山——的神，说：这座山的神，我空手就能把他抓住。他登上山去，在山坡上遇见一头白色的野猪，大得像一头牛。倭建命大声说：这头变成白猪的，是山神的使者吧。现在不杀它，等我回来的时候再杀。说完就继续往上走。可是那头白猪并不是使者，正是山神本人。因为他说了这样的大话，山神降下了猛烈的冰雹，打得倭建命神志昏迷。这把一路保护他的剑，此刻已经不在他的身边。书中还记下，宴会上倭建命看见她外衣的下摆沾着月事的血迹，便以歌相问，她以歌相答，说日月流转，等他等得月亮都已升起。伊吹山在今天滋贺与岐阜的交界处。后世注释家认为，倭建命对着山神口出狂言——原文称为“言举”——正是招来灾祸的原因。

From Kai Yamato Takeru crossed Shinano, subdued the deity of the Shinano pass, and came back to Owari, to Miyazu-hime's house, to keep his pledge. They exchanged songs and were married. Then he left the Kusanagi sword with Miyazu-hime and went bare-handed to take the deity of Mount Ibuki, saying: 'As for the deity of this mountain, I will take him with my bare hands.' Climbing the mountain, he met on its slope a white boar as big as an ox. He said aloud: 'This one that has turned into a white boar must be the messenger of the mountain deity. I will not kill it now; I will kill it on my way back,' and went on climbing. But the white boar was no messenger; it was the deity himself. Because of these boastful words the deity sent down a great storm of hail that struck Yamato Takeru senseless. The sword that had protected him all the way was no longer at his side. The text also tells that at the feast he saw the stain of her monthly blood on the hem of her robe and questioned her in song, and she answered in song that months had passed and the moon had risen while she waited for him. Mount Ibuki lies on the border of present-day Shiga and Gifu. Later commentators see his boastful speech against the deity — kotoage, speaking out — as what brought the disaster on him.

> 证据说明：《古事记》张伯伦译本第八十七至八十八节。

### 11 · 三重的腿 / Legs like a triple-bent rice cake

倭建命下了山，来到玉仓部的清泉边休息，神志稍稍清醒了一些，所以那眼泉被叫作居寤清水。他继续往前走，到了当艺野，说：我的心本来总想在天空中飞翔，可现在我的脚却走不动了，变得蹒跚踉跄。所以那里叫作当艺。他又拄着手杖慢慢地走，那段坡就叫杖衡坂。到了尾津岬的一棵孤松下，他发现以前在这里吃饭时忘下的一把刀，竟然还在原处。他唱道：尾津岬那棵孤松啊，我的兄长，你若是人，我就给你佩上刀，给你穿上衣服。到了三重村，他说：我的腿像三重弯曲的年糕一样，累极了。所以那里叫作三重。书中一路记下他留下的地名，像是记下了他越来越沉重的脚步。当艺在今天的岐阜一带，尾津岬在伊势的桑名附近，据说今天三重县的名字也与这里有关。那棵孤松下的刀，原来一直在等着它的主人。

Coming down the mountain, he rested by the clear spring of Tamakurabe and his mind cleared a little, so the spring is called the Awakening Spring. He went on to the moor of Tagi and said: 'My heart always longed to fly through the sky, but now my feet will not walk; they stagger.' So the place is called Tagi. Leaning on a staff he walked slowly on, and the slope is called the Staff Slope. At the single pine on Cape Otsu he found a sword he had left there once while eating, still lying where he had left it, and he sang: single pine of Cape Otsu, my elder brother — if you were a man, I would gird you with a sword and clothe you. At the village of Mie he said: 'My legs are like a rice cake bent three times; I am utterly weary.' So the place is called Mie. The text records the place-names he left behind, as if marking his ever heavier steps. Tagi lies in present-day Gifu and Cape Otsu near Kuwana in Ise; the name of Mie Prefecture is said to come from this place. The sword under the lone pine had been waiting all along for its master.

> 证据说明：《古事记》张伯伦译本第八十九节。

### 12 · 思国之歌与白鸟 / The songs of home and the white bird

到了能烦野，倭建命思念故乡，唱道：大和是国中最好的地方，层层叠叠的青山像垣墙一样环绕，大和啊，多么美好。又唱：愿那些性命完好的人，把平群山上的熊白梼叶插在头上。又唱：啊，从我家那边升起的云啊。病势沉重的时候，他唱了最后一首歌：我把那把大刀留在了少女的床边——那把刀啊！唱完就死了。使者把消息报告给京城。他的妃子和孩子们赶来，在那里修了陵墓，在周围的稻田里爬来爬去，哭着唱歌。这时，倭建命化作一只八寻长的白鸟，飞上天空，向海边飞去。妃子和孩子们在后面追，赤脚被竹子的残茬划破也不觉得痛，一边追一边唱。书中说，这四首歌都在他的葬礼上唱过，所以直到今天，天皇的大葬还唱这些歌。白鸟飞到河内的志几停了下来，人们在那里修了陵墓，叫作白鸟御陵。可是白鸟又从那里飞上天空，飞走了。

At the moor of Nobo, longing for his home, he sang: Yamato is the best of lands; the green mountains fold round it like a fence; Yamato, how lovely. And: let those whose lives are whole put leaves of the bear-oak of Mount Heguri in their hair. And: ah, the clouds rising from the direction of my home. When his sickness grew grave he sang a last song: by the maiden's bed I left my great sword — that sword! — and when he had sung it he died. Messengers carried the news to the capital. His consorts and children came down, made a tomb there, and crawled about the rice fields around it, weeping and singing. Then Yamato Takeru turned into a great white bird eight fathoms long, rose into the sky and flew towards the shore. His consorts and children followed, their bare feet cut on bamboo stubble, feeling no pain, singing as they went. The text says these four songs were all sung at his funeral, and so they are sung at the great funerals of the Heavenly Sovereigns to this day. The white bird stopped at Shiki in Kawachi, and a tomb was made there called the Tomb of the White Bird. But from there it rose into the sky again and flew away.

> 证据说明：《古事记》张伯伦译本第八十九至九十节。
