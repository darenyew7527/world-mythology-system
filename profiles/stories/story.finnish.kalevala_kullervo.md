## 阅读边界

温塔莫与卡勒沃两兄弟因为一连串小事结下深仇，温塔莫杀光了卡勒沃的族人，只留下一个怀孕的女子做奴隶。她生下的库勒沃在摇篮里就发誓复仇。温塔莫三次想杀死他：把他装进桶里扔进湖中，扔进火堆，吊在橡树上，他都活了下来。温塔莫让他干活，他什么都干坏，最后被卖给铁匠伊尔马里宁做奴隶。伊尔马里宁的妻子让他去放牛，在他的面包里烤进一块石头，他父亲留下的唯一遗物——一把刀——在石头上折断了。库勒沃把狼和熊变成牛群赶回家，主妇在挤奶时被它们咬死。他逃进森林，找到了还活着的父母，却在替父亲交税回来的路上诱骗了一个少女，后来才知道她是自己失散多年的妹妹，妹妹投入急流而死。库勒沃出征，灭了温塔莫一族，回到家时亲人都已死去。他来到妹妹遇害的地方，问自己的剑是否愿意饮他的血，然后伏剑而死。

The brothers Untamo and Kalervo fall into a deep feud over a run of petty quarrels; Untamo slays Kalervo's people, sparing only a pregnant woman as a slave. Her son Kullervo vows vengeance in the cradle. Untamo tries three times to kill him — in a barrel thrown into the lake, on a pyre, hanged from an oak — and each time he lives. Every task Kullervo is given he ruins, and at last he is sold as a slave to the smith Ilmarinen. Ilmarinen's wife sends him to herd the cattle and bakes a stone into his bread, on which his father's knife, the only keepsake of his kin, breaks. Kullervo drives home wolves and bears in the shape of cattle, and the mistress is killed as she milks. Fleeing, he finds his parents alive, but on his way home from paying his father's tax he seduces a maiden who proves to be his long-lost sister; she throws herself into the rapids. Kullervo goes to war, destroys Untamo's clan, and comes home to find his family dead. At the place where he wronged his sister he asks his sword whether it will drink his blood, and falls upon it.

### 01 · 两兄弟的仇恨 / The brothers' feud

从前，有一位母亲养大了一窝小鸡，一只被带到了俄罗斯，一只被带到了卡累利阿，还有一只留在家里——诗人用这样的比喻开始讲述两兄弟的故事。哥哥温塔莫和弟弟卡勒沃住得很近，却总是合不来。他们为了渔场、为了田地和羊群，为了一连串鸡毛蒜皮的小事争吵不休，怨恨越积越深。终于，温塔莫召集了一群武士，带着刀剑去攻打卡勒沃。卡勒沃的族人全被杀光，房屋被烧成灰烬，只剩下一个怀了孕的女子活了下来，被温塔莫带回家去，做了打扫屋子、劈柴担水的奴隶。在温塔莫看来，卡勒沃一族已经从世上消失了。可是那个女子腹中的孩子，正是后来让温塔莫一族灭亡的人。在隆洛特编排的《卡勒瓦拉》里，库勒沃的这几章最为阴暗，它们主要取材于卡累利阿和英格里亚一带的民间唱本。二十世纪的作家托尔金也深受这个故事的触动，写下了《库勒沃的故事》。

Once, the poem begins, a mother raised a brood of chicks: one was carried to Russia, one to Karelia, and one stayed at home — with such an image the singer opens the tale of two brothers. The elder, Untamo, and the younger, Kalervo, lived near each other but could never agree. They quarrelled over fishing-grounds, over fields and flocks, over a run of petty matters, and their grudge grew deeper and deeper. At last Untamo gathered a band of warriors and attacked Kalervo with swords. Kalervo's people were all slain and his houses burned to ashes; only one woman, who was with child, survived, and Untamo took her home as a slave to sweep the house, split wood and carry water. To Untamo, Kalervo's line had vanished from the earth. But the child in that woman's womb would one day destroy Untamo's own clan. In Lönnrot's Kalevala these runes are the darkest; they draw chiefly on songs from Karelia and Ingria. In the twentieth century J. R. R. Tolkien was moved by the tale to write his own Story of Kullervo.

### 02 · 摇篮里的复仇者 / The avenger in the cradle

那个女子在温塔莫家里生下了一个男孩，母亲给他取名叫库勒沃，温塔莫的人则叫他“战争之子”。孩子还在摇篮里的时候，就显出了惊人的力气。到了第三个月，他一脚踢开了包着自己的襁褓，把摇篮的底都蹬破了，大家都说：这孩子将来一定会成为一个强壮的人。可是库勒沃一天天长大，心里只有一个念头。有一次，温塔莫听见这个摇篮里的孩子说：等我长大了，有了力气，我要为父亲报仇，为母亲流过的眼泪报仇。温塔莫听了，心里一惊，对家人说：这孩子留着，将来就是我们一族的祸害。他决定趁孩子还小，把他除掉。这个出生在仇人家里的孩子，从一开始就被仇恨包围着。在芬兰语的原诗里，他常被连名带姓地称为“卡勒沃之子库勒沃”，仿佛他的名字本身就背负着父亲一族的命运。这样的话出自一个还在吃奶的孩子之口，温塔莫一家都不寒而栗。

In Untamo's house the woman bore a son. His mother named him Kullervo, and Untamo's people called him the son of war. While still in the cradle he showed astonishing strength: in his third month he kicked off his swaddling clothes and broke the bottom of the cradle, and everyone said he would grow to be a mighty man. But as Kullervo grew, one thought filled his heart. Once Untamo heard the child in the cradle say: 'When I am grown and strong, I will avenge my father and the tears my mother has shed.' Untamo was startled and said to his household: 'If this child lives, he will be the ruin of our clan.' He resolved to be rid of him while he was still small. Born in his enemy's house, the child was surrounded by hatred from the start. In the Finnish poem he is often called by his full name, Kullervo son of Kalervo, as though his very name carried the fate of his father's people. Such words from a child still at the breast chilled Untamo's whole household.

### 03 · 杀不死的孩子 / The child who could not be killed

温塔莫先叫人做了一只木桶，把库勒沃装进去，扔进了湖里。过了三个晚上，人们去看，只见木桶浮在水面上，库勒沃坐在桶上，手里拿着一根铜钓竿，正在钓鱼。温塔莫又叫人砍来一大堆桦木和松木，堆成一座高高的柴堆，把库勒沃扔进火里烧。过了三天，人们去看，只见柴堆已经烧成了灰，库勒沃坐在灰烬里，拿着一根拨火棍，正在拨弄余火，头发一根也没有烧焦。温塔莫又叫人把孩子吊在一棵橡树上。过了三天三夜，人们去看，库勒沃没有死，他坐在树枝上，正在用小刀在树干上刻画，刻的全是手拿刀剑的武士。温塔莫无可奈何，只好说：这孩子杀不死，那就让他做奴隶，替我们干活吧。水、火和绳索都没能杀死他，像是在说这个孩子身上有一种无法消灭的力量；可这股力量后来带给他和身边的人的，却只有灾难。

First Untamo had a barrel made, shut Kullervo inside and threw him into the lake. After three nights his men went to look: the barrel was floating on the water, and Kullervo sat on top of it with a copper rod in his hand, fishing. Next Untamo had a great pile of birch and pine logs heaped up and threw Kullervo into the fire. After three days they went to look: the pile had burned to ashes, and Kullervo sat in the ashes with a poker, stirring the embers, not a hair singed. Then Untamo had the child hanged from an oak. After three days and nights they went to look: Kullervo was not dead but sat on a branch carving pictures on the trunk with a little knife — all of warriors with swords in their hands. Helpless, Untamo said: 'If this child cannot be killed, let him be a slave and work for us.' Water, fire and rope could not kill him, as if the child held a strength nothing could destroy; yet that strength would bring only disaster to him and to those around him.

### 04 · 无用的仆人 / The useless servant

温塔莫给库勒沃派活。先让他照看一个婴儿，他却把婴儿弄伤、弄死了，还把摇篮烧掉；诗中这一段说得很直接，本篇只交代结果。温塔莫又让他去砍林开荒，他把整片森林都砍倒了，然后诅咒那块地永远不长庄稼、不长树木。温塔莫让他筑篱笆，他把整棵整棵的松树和冷杉竖起来，筑起一道高到天上、没有缝隙也没有门的围墙，谁也进不去出不来。温塔莫让他打黑麦，他把黑麦打成了粉末和糠皮。温塔莫说：这个孩子什么活都干不好，留着他只会带来灾祸。他就把库勒沃卖到了卡累利阿，卖给了铁匠伊尔马里宁，作价不过是几口旧锅和一些破旧的农具。库勒沃就这样从仇人的奴隶，变成了陌生人家的奴隶。库勒沃并不是懒惰，他力大无穷，只是心里装满了怨恨，每一件活计到了他手里，都变成了破坏。伊尔马里宁并不知道，自己买回家的是怎样一个人。

Untamo set Kullervo to work. First he was told to mind a baby; he hurt it and it died, and he burned the cradle — the poem tells it bluntly, and this retelling gives only the outcome. Next he was sent to clear the forest; he felled the whole forest and cursed the land so that neither grain nor tree would ever grow there. Told to build a fence, he set up whole pines and firs into a wall reaching the sky, with no gap and no gate, so that no one could get in or out. Told to thresh the rye, he beat it to dust and chaff. Untamo said: 'This boy can do nothing right; keeping him will only bring misfortune.' So he sold Kullervo into Karelia, to the smith Ilmarinen, for no more than a few worn-out kettles and some battered farm tools. So Kullervo passed from slave in his enemy's house to slave in a stranger's. Kullervo was not idle; he was enormously strong, but so full of resentment that every task in his hands turned to destruction. Ilmarinen did not know what kind of man he had brought home.

### 05 · 藏着石头的面包 / The bread with a stone in it

伊尔马里宁的妻子，就是当年那位波赫约拉的少女，如今是铁匠家的女主人。她看着这个新来的奴隶，心里想：让他去放牛吧。她要给库勒沃准备路上吃的干粮，就烤了一块面包：上面是燕麦，下面是小麦，中间却藏了一块石头。她把面包交给库勒沃，叮嘱他：等牛群进了树林，你再吃这块面包。然后，她向乌戈、森林的主人和森林之母念了很长的祈祷，请他们保佑牛群平安，不受狼和熊的伤害，又念起对熊的咒语，请熊不要伤害她的牛。库勒沃赶着牛群出了门，在沼泽和林间放牧，一路上抱怨自己的命运：别人吃的是白面包，喝的是麦酒，而他这个奴隶，只能吃干硬的面包，在荒野里放牛。原诗里，她的祈祷占了这一章的大半篇幅，保存了古代芬兰牧人祈求牲畜平安、驱赶猛兽的咒语。读者也就更明白，后面她的牛群为什么会在狼和熊的手里遭殃。

Ilmarinen's wife — the maiden of Pohjola of old, now mistress of the smith's house — looked at the new slave and thought: let him herd the cattle. To provide for him she baked a loaf: oats on top, wheat below, and in the middle a stone. She gave it to Kullervo and told him not to eat it until the herd had gone into the woods. Then she spoke long prayers to Ukko and to the master and mother of the forest, asking them to keep the herd safe from wolf and bear, and chanted the charms to the bear, begging it to spare her cattle. Kullervo drove the herd out and pastured them through swamp and wood, lamenting his lot as he went: others ate white bread and drank ale, while he, a slave, had only hard bread and the cattle in the wilderness. In the poem her prayers fill most of the rune, preserving the charms with which old Finnish herders asked safety for their cattle and kept off wild beasts — which makes plain why her herd later comes to grief by wolf and bear.

### 06 · 断刀 / The broken knife

到了中午，库勒沃坐在山坡上，拿出主妇给的面包，用刀一切，刀刃碰到了中间的石头，啪的一声折断了。这把刀是他父亲留下的唯一遗物，是他与被杀光的卡勒沃一族之间仅剩的联系。库勒沃看着断刀，哭了起来，说：这是我唯一的珍宝，是我父亲的刀，如今竟断在一个女主人的石头上。一只乌鸦从树上对他说：你这卡勒沃的孩子，为什么这样悲伤？把牛群赶进沼泽吧，让狼和熊把它们都吃掉，再把狼和熊召来，让它们变成牛的样子，傍晚赶回家去。库勒沃照着乌鸦的话做了：他把牛群赶进沼泽，让狼和熊吃掉了它们，又用咒语把狼变成了牛，把熊变成了母牛，赶着这群野兽，一路吹着号角，向主人家走去。这一刀断下去，断的不只是一把刀，也是库勒沃心中最后的一点忍耐。他把断刀收好，心里只想着怎样报复那个女主人。

At noon Kullervo sat down on a hillside, took out the mistress's loaf and cut into it; the blade struck the stone in the middle and snapped. The knife was the only keepsake left by his father, his last link with the slaughtered people of Kalervo. Kullervo looked at the broken knife and wept: 'This was my only treasure, my father's knife, and it has broken on a mistress's stone.' A crow called to him from a tree: 'Child of Kalervo, why so sad? Drive the herd into the swamp and let the wolves and bears devour them; then call the wolves and bears, give them the shape of cattle, and drive them home at evening.' Kullervo did as the crow said: he drove the herd into the swamp to be devoured, then by his charms turned the wolves into oxen and the bears into cows, and drove the beasts towards his master's house, blowing his horn. With the blade broke the last of Kullervo's patience. He put the broken knife away, thinking only of how to repay the mistress.

### 07 · 主妇之死 / The death of the mistress

傍晚，库勒沃把那群“牛”赶进了牛栏，号角声响彻四方。伊尔马里宁的妻子高兴地说：感谢乌戈，牛群平安回来了。她提着奶桶去挤奶，俯下身去，那头“母牛”却原来是一只熊，扑上来把她撕咬。她惊叫着向库勒沃求救：好心的牧人啊，救救我，拿开这些野兽吧，我会给你最好的衣服，最好的面包。库勒沃说：你给我吃石头做的面包，现在你自己尝尝苦头吧。她又向乌戈祈求，请他用箭射死库勒沃。可是没有人来救她，伊尔马里宁的妻子就这样死在了牛栏里。这位曾经在彩虹上织布、让万奈莫宁和伊尔马里宁都为之倾倒的少女，就因为一块藏在面包里的石头，丢掉了性命。库勒沃吹起号角，离开了铁匠的家，逃进了森林。伊尔马里宁后来失去了妻子，悲伤不已。他为她哭了许多日夜；后面的诗章会讲到，他甚至想用金子和银子为自己打造一个新的妻子。

At evening Kullervo drove his 'cattle' into the byre, the horn sounding far and wide. Ilmarinen's wife said joyfully: 'Thanks be to Ukko, the herd has come home safe.' She took her milking pail and bent to milk, but the 'cow' was a bear, which sprang on her and tore her. She shrieked to Kullervo for help: 'Good herdsman, save me, drive off these beasts, and I will give you the best clothes and the best bread.' Kullervo answered: 'You fed me bread made of stone; now taste your own bitterness.' She prayed to Ukko to shoot Kullervo with his arrows. But no one came to save her, and Ilmarinen's wife died in the byre. The maiden who once wove on the rainbow, for whom Väinämöinen and Ilmarinen had both longed, lost her life over a stone hidden in a loaf. Kullervo blew his horn, left the smith's house and fled into the forest. Ilmarinen was left to grieve for his wife. He wept for her many nights; a later rune tells how he even tried to forge himself a new wife of gold and silver.

### 08 · 找到父母 / Finding his parents

库勒沃在森林里游荡，心中充满了悲伤和愤怒。他想：我该到哪里去？我是谁的孩子？我从来没有一个家。他决定去找温塔莫报仇。在林中，他遇见一个穿着蓝衣的老妇人，她对他说：卡勒沃的孩子，你的父亲并没有死，他还活着，你的母亲也活着，他们就住在拉普兰的边上，靠近一片湖。库勒沃半信半疑地出发了，走了很久，终于在湖边找到了一座房子，见到了自己的父亲和母亲。母亲哭着说：我的儿子，我以为你早就死了，还为你流了许多眼泪。我还以为你是被卖到远方去了。他们告诉他，他还有一个姐姐，可是很多年前，她到林子里去采浆果，就再也没有回来，谁也不知道她去了哪里。这是库勒沃一生中少有的温暖时刻：他终于有了一个家，有了父亲和母亲。可那个失踪的姐姐，却像一道阴影，留在了故事里。

Kullervo wandered in the forest, full of grief and anger. 'Where shall I go? Whose child am I? I have never had a home.' He resolved to go and take vengeance on Untamo. In the woods he met an old woman in a blue robe, who said: 'Child of Kalervo, your father is not dead. He lives, and your mother lives too; they dwell on the borders of Lapland, near a lake.' Half believing, Kullervo set out, and after long wandering found a house by a lake and saw his father and mother. His mother wept: 'My son, I thought you long dead and shed many tears for you; I thought you had been sold far away.' They told him that he had an elder sister, but that years ago she had gone into the woods to pick berries and never come back, and no one knew where she had gone. It is one of the few warm moments of Kullervo's life: at last he had a home, a father and a mother. But the lost sister lingers in the story like a shadow.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三十四章。

### 09 · 路上的少女 / The maiden on the road

回到家里，库勒沃想帮父母干活，却什么也干不好：父亲让他划船，他把船桨划断了，把船底都划穿了；让他撒网捕鱼，他把水搅成了泥浆，把渔网撕成了碎片。父亲只好派他去交税。库勒沃交完了税，赶着雪橇回家，在路上遇见一个少女，请她坐上自己的雪橇，她不肯；又遇见一个少女，也不肯。第三次，他遇见一个在荒野里赶路的少女，就用银子和漂亮的布料引诱她，把她拉上了雪橇。两人在雪橇上过了一夜。第二天早晨，少女问他是哪一家的人。库勒沃说：我是卡勒沃的儿子。少女听了，脸色大变，说：我也是卡勒沃的女儿，就是多年前在林子里采浆果时迷了路的那个女儿。他们谁也不知道对方是谁，两个失散多年的孩子，就在这样的误会里相遇了。这一夜，成了两个人一生中最大的不幸。

Back home, Kullervo tried to help his parents but could do nothing right. Set to row, he broke the oars and drove the boat's bottom through; set to fish, he churned the water to mud and tore the net to shreds. So his father sent him to pay the taxes. Driving home in his sledge after paying them, he met a maiden on the road and asked her into his sledge; she refused. He met another, who also refused. The third time he met a maiden travelling across the wilds, and with silver and fine cloth he lured her and drew her into the sledge. They spent the night together. In the morning the maiden asked him what family he came from. Kullervo said: 'I am the son of Kalervo.' At this her face changed, and she said: 'I too am Kalervo's daughter — the daughter who lost her way picking berries in the woods long ago.' Neither knew who the other was; two children lost for years met in this terrible ignorance. That night became the greatest misfortune of both their lives.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三十五章。

### 10 · 急流 / The rapids

少女明白了一切，悲痛欲绝。她从雪橇上跳下来，奔向河边那奔腾的急流，说：我宁愿让急流把我卷走，也不愿再活在这世上。她投身于激流之中，被汹涌的河水吞没了。库勒沃站在河边，悔恨交加，他说：我做了什么样的事啊！我毁了我的妹妹，我毁了我自己，我这一生只给别人带来不幸。他一度想就此结束自己的生命。他回到家里，把这件事告诉了母亲。母亲劝他说：不要死，我的儿子，你可以远远地走开，到一个没有人认识你的地方去，等时间慢慢平息你的悲伤。可是库勒沃说：在我死之前，还有一件事要做。我要去找温塔莫，为我父亲的族人报仇。诗中说她在投水之前留下了悲伤的话，哀叹自己生来就不幸。库勒沃的一生，从仇人家的摇篮开始，到这里，已经只剩下了复仇和悔恨。

The maiden understood everything and was overcome with grief. She leapt from the sledge and ran to the roaring rapids of the river, crying that she would rather be swept away by the torrent than live on in the world. She threw herself into the rapids and was swallowed by the raging waters. Kullervo stood by the river, torn with remorse: 'What have I done! I have ruined my sister and ruined myself; all my life I have brought only misfortune on others.' For a while he thought of ending his own life. He went home and told his mother. She pleaded: 'Do not die, my son. Go far away, to a place where no one knows you, and let time ease your grief.' But Kullervo said: 'Before I die there is one thing left to do. I will go to Untamo and avenge my father's people.' The poem gives her sorrowful words before she leaps, lamenting that she was born to misfortune. Kullervo's life, begun in an enemy's cradle, now held nothing but vengeance and remorse.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三十五章。

### 11 · 出征与复仇 / War and vengeance

库勒沃准备出征，他一个个地问家人：我死了，你们会为我哭泣吗？父亲说：不会，我会再生一个比你更好的儿子。哥哥说：不会，我会找一个比你更好的兄弟。姐妹说：不会，我会找一个比你更好的兄弟。只有母亲说：我会为你哭泣，我会在屋里哭，在田里哭，在每一个地方哭，哭到我的眼泪流干。库勒沃上了路。打仗的途中，一个个使者跑来告诉他：你的父亲死了，你的哥哥死了，你的姐妹死了。他都不肯回去，只说：让他们安葬吧。最后使者说：你的母亲死了。他才流下了眼泪。他向乌戈祈求一把好剑，乌戈给了他。库勒沃来到温塔莫的家，把温塔莫的族人全部杀死，烧毁了他们的房屋，只留下一片灰烬和石头。父亲的仇，终于报了。全家人里，只有母亲的爱从未改变。可是对库勒沃来说，复仇并没有带来安慰，回家时迎接他的只有冷清。

As he made ready for war, Kullervo asked each of his family: 'When I die, will you weep for me?' His father said: 'No; I will beget a better son.' His brother said: 'No; I will find a better brother.' His sister said: 'No; I will find a better brother.' Only his mother said: 'I will weep for you, in the house and in the fields and everywhere, until my tears run dry.' Kullervo set out. On the way messengers came one after another: your father is dead, your brother is dead, your sister is dead. He would not turn back, saying only: let them be buried. At last a messenger said: your mother is dead. Then he wept. He prayed to Ukko for a good sword, and Ukko gave it. Coming to Untamo's house, Kullervo slew all of Untamo's people and burned their dwellings, leaving only ashes and stones. His father's death was avenged at last. Of all his family, only his mother's love had never changed. Yet vengeance brought Kullervo no comfort; only emptiness awaited him at home.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三十六章。

### 12 · 卡勒沃之子的结局 / The end of Kalervo's son

库勒沃回到家里，家中空无一人，炉灶冰冷，父亲、母亲、兄弟和姐妹都已经死去。他在母亲的坟前哭泣，母亲在坟墓里对他说：带上我们的黑狗穆斯蒂，到森林里去吧，向森林的神灵求一个安身的地方。库勒沃带着狗走进森林，不知不觉来到了当年他诱骗妹妹的那片荒野。那里的草不再生长，树叶凋零，仿佛大地也在为那件事哀悼。库勒沃拔出剑来，问它：我的剑啊，你愿意饮一个有罪之人的血吗？剑回答说：我连无辜者的血都饮过，为什么不能饮你这有罪之人的血？库勒沃把剑柄插在地上，剑尖朝上，扑倒在剑上死去了。诗的最后，老万奈莫宁听说了这一切，说：以后的人们啊，不要用错误的方式抚养孩子，不要让孩子在陌生人的摇篮里长大，否则他永远学不会正直。卡勒沃之子库勒沃的故事，就这样结束了。

Kullervo came home to find the house empty and the hearth cold; father, mother, brother and sister were all dead. He wept at his mother's grave, and from the grave his mother said: 'Take our black dog Musti and go into the forest, and ask the spirits of the forest for a place to rest.' Kullervo went into the forest with the dog and came, without knowing it, to the very wilds where he had wronged his sister. The grass there no longer grew and the leaves had withered, as if the earth itself mourned what had happened. Kullervo drew his sword and asked it: 'My sword, will you drink the blood of a guilty man?' The sword answered: 'I have drunk the blood of the innocent; why should I not drink yours, who are guilty?' Kullervo set the hilt in the ground, point upward, and fell upon it and died. At the end of the rune old Väinämöinen hears of it all and says: people of the future, never raise a child wrongly, never let him grow up in a stranger's cradle, or he will never learn to be upright. So ended the story of Kullervo, son of Kalervo.

> 证据说明：《卡勒瓦拉》克劳福德译本第三十六章。
