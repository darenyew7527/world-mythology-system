## Claims 与证据

- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.01` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', records the common belief that summer thunder that splits trees and wrecks houses is Heaven taking a dragon, and that thunder which kills a man is Heaven's anger punishing a hidden fault, such as feeding people unclean food, the rumbling being Heaven's angry voice.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), opening: the common belief
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.02` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', argues that since the same thunder at the same moment both splits trees and kills people, it cannot be both Heaven auspiciously taking a dragon and Heaven angrily punishing a fault, and asks what fault a dragon has that Heaven should be angry.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), the argument against Heaven's anger
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.03` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', concludes that a man under a tree or in a house who is struck by thunder has merely been hit by chance, and so denies that thunder singles people out for their faults.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), people struck by chance (conclusion)
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.04` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', says that painters depicted thunder as linked drums and a strongman called Leigong pulling the drums with his left hand and raising a mallet with his right, the rumbling being the drums and the crack the mallet, and calls the image false.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), the painters' image of Leigong
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.05` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', objects that thunder is a sound or breath that cannot be made into drums, asks where Leigong's drums hang and where he stands, and argues that since painters give flying immortals wings, a flying Leigong drawn without wings is a contradiction.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), objections to the painted Leigong
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.06` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', gives five proofs that thunder is fire: the singed hair, burned skin and smell of fire on those it kills; red-hot stones thrown into wells; the rumbling of a chilled belly; the fire-like flash of lightning; and the burning of houses and plants.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), the five proofs that thunder is fire
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.07` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', compares thunder to a peck of water poured on a smelting furnace, which bursts with a thunderous crack, calling heaven and earth a great furnace, yang energy its fire and clouds and rain its water.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), the furnace comparison
- `claim.v0360.leigong_leixu.08` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Wang Chong, Lunheng, 'Lei Xu', concludes that thunder is the surging energy of great yang, beginning in the first month, fiercest in the fifth and hidden in autumn and winter, produced when yin and yang contend in summer, so that it kills, breaks or wrecks whatever it happens to strike.
  - 来源：[Lunheng, Lei Xu](https://ctext.org/lunheng/lei-xu/zh)；定位：Lunheng, Lei Xu (雷虚), conclusion: the energy of great yang
