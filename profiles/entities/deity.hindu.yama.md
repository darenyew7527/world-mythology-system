## 概要

God of death and of dharma, who comes himself with his noose for Satyavān's life and, moved by Sāvitrī's words as she follows him, grants her boons until he restores her husband (Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, Pativratā-māhātmya; also a suitor god in the Nala story).

## Claims 与证据

- `claim.v0350.mbh_savitri.09` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, Section CCXCV says that as Satyavān lay with his head in Sāvitrī's lap, Yama, red-clad and crowned with a noose in his hand, came himself because of Satyavān's virtue and drew out his thumb-sized soul.
  - 来源：[The Mahabharata of Krishna-Dwaipayana Vyasa, tr. Kisari Mohan Ganguli (1883–1896)](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm)；定位：Vana Parva, Pativrata-mahatmya Parva, Section CCXCV
- `claim.v0350.mbh_savitri.11` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Mahābhārata, Vana Parva, Section CCXCV says Yama granted Sāvitrī further boons — her father-in-law's kingdom, a hundred sons for her father and a hundred sons by Satyavān — and, since the last could not be fulfilled without Satyavān, loosened his noose and restored her husband.
  - 来源：[The Mahabharata of Krishna-Dwaipayana Vyasa, tr. Kisari Mohan Ganguli (1883–1896)](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm)；定位：Vana Parva, Pativrata-mahatmya Parva, Section CCXCV
