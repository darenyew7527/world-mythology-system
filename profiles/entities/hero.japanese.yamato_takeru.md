- `claim.v0280.japan.yamato_atsuta_event` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.88] The official shrine history links Yamato Takeru's death after leaving the sword in Hikami to the Atsuta enshrinement account.
  - 来源：[Atsuta Jingu: Introduction](https://www.atsutajingu.or.jp/en/intro/)；定位：Introduction — History
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.02` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Section LXXX says Wo-usu, sent west against the Kumaso bravoes, took his aunt Yamato-hime's robe and skirt, let down his hair like a girl's and entered their housewarming feast, where they seated him between them.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXX, Emperor Kei-kō (Part V.—Yamato-take Slays the Kumaso Bravoes) (kj087)
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.06` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Section LXXXIII says that when the ruler of Sagamu set fire to the moor around him, Yamato Takeru opened his aunt's bag, mowed the grass with his sword and lit a counter-fire with the fire-striker, then killed the rulers, so that the place is called Yaizu.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXXIII, Emperor Kei-kō (Part VIII.—Yamato-take Slays the Rulers of Sagamu) (kj090)
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.08` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Section LXXXV says Yamato Takeru killed the deity of the Ashigara pass, which came as a white deer, with a wild chive, and on the pass sighed three times 'Azuma haya', so that the land is called Azuma.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXXV, Emperor Kei-kō (Part X.—Yamato-take Slays the Deity of the Ashigara Pass) (kj092)
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.09` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Section LXXXVI says that at the palace of Sakaori in Kai, when Yamato Takeru asked in verse how many nights had passed since Niibari and Tsukuba, an old fire-keeper answered nine nights and ten days, and was made ruler of the East.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXXVI (kj093)
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.11` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Section LXXXIX says Yamato Takeru, weakened, named the Awakening Spring, Tagi and the Staff Slope, found his forgotten sword still under the pine of Cape Otsu, and at Mie said his legs were like a thrice-bent rice cake.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXXIX, Emperor Kei-kō (Part XIV.—The Death of Yamato-take) (kj096)
- `claim.v0350.kojiki_yamato_takeru.12` [VERIFIED / TEXT_SAYS / 0.90] Kojiki Sections LXXXIX–XC say Yamato Takeru died at Nobo after singing of his longing for Yamato and of the sword left by the maiden's bed, and that he turned into a great white bird that flew towards the shore, stopped at Shiki and rose into the sky again.
  - 来源：[The Kojiki: Records of Ancient Matters (tr. Basil Hall Chamberlain)](https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm)；定位：Sect. LXXXIX–XC, Emperor Kei-kō (Parts XIV–XV) (kj096–kj097)
